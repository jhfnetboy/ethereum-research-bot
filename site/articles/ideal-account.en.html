Scope: the 365 daily reports on this site from 2025-11-12 to 2026-11-11, plus the original sources they cite. Statuses are as of 2026-11-11 (UTC+7). Each claim is followed by a link to the matching daily report on this site, then to the original source. Status labels: live means activated on Ethereum mainnet; settled means Final or Scheduled for Inclusion (SFI); candidate means Considered for Inclusion (CFI), in an upgrade's scope but still open to change; proposed means Draft or Proposed for Inclusion (PFI); declined means DFI or rejected on a call; withdrawn means Withdrawn. Anything marked "our recommendation" is our own reading of the sources, not a decision by any team.

Key takeaways

  1. You can build something close to the ideal account today, but you have to assemble it. EIP-7702, which lets an EOA run contract code, shipped with Pectra {{c:2026-02-18|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/18/protocol-priorities-update-2026}}. ERC-4337 reached Final on 2026-06-02, locking in the EntryPoint, bundler and paymaster interfaces {{c:2026-06-02|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1773}}. ERC-7913, which lets accounts verify keys that have no Ethereum address (passkeys or post-quantum keys, for example), reached Final on 2025-12-16 {{c:2025-12-16|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1213}}.
  2. On L1, native AA will come through EIP-8141 frame transactions. EIP-8141 was Scheduled for Inclusion in Hegotá on 2026-08-27, with the caveat that the implementation, and even the EIP number, may still change {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}}. ACDE #245 made it the Hegotá headliner on 2026-09-10 {{c:2026-09-10|https://x.com/vdWijden/status/2098068285791121842}} {{c:2026-09-11|https://x.com/ethrex_client/status/2098440608922608110}}. Base has said it will roll out EIP-8130 on its L2 {{c:2026-08-21|https://x.com/jessepollak/status/2090844327748796902}}, and 8130's transaction type was later split out as EIP-8430 {{c:2026-10-08|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12386}}.
  3. Privacy will come from protocol primitives that apps build on, not from privacy built into the protocol. EIP-8250 keyed nonces, EIP-8272 recent roots and EIP-7906 transaction assertions became Hegotá candidates (CFI) on 2026-09-24 {{c:2026-09-24|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12377}}. EIP-8182, which would have enshrined private transfers, was declined (DFI) on 2026-09-10 {{c:2026-09-10|https://x.com/vdWijden/status/2098068285791121842}}.
  4. The year's biggest account security incident came down to authorization paths, not cryptography. The Binance incident on 2026-10-29 affected about $6.3B {{c:2026-10-30|https://ethereal.news/ethereal-news-weekly-45/}}. The preliminary report says that once the attacker controlled the signing service, it took 50 transactions to set up a malicious 7702 delegation, and revoking it took longer because the wallets used MPC {{c:2026-11-03|https://x.com/binance/status/2117626126214504729}}. lightclient, one of the 7702 authors, called 7702 "just an accelerant for an existing attack" {{c:2026-11-03|https://x.com/dapplion/status/2117362449942708670}}.
  5. Post-quantum readiness so far means "swappable and aggregatable", with no scheme chosen. EIP-8141 moved signatures into their own list so they can be aggregated later, post-quantum ones included; that change merged on 2026-05-22 {{c:2026-05-22|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11481}}. EIP-8288 (a frame type for aggregating post-quantum signatures and STARKs) and EIP-8425 (quantum freeze and account recovery) are both still drafts {{c:2026-06-06|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11772}} {{c:2026-10-24|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12383}}.

Putting it together

Path A: what works today (our recommendation).

  1. Keep the EOA address as the entry point and use EIP-7702 to delegate to an audited ERC-7579 modular account. 7702 is live and 4337 is Final {{c:2026-02-18|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/18/protocol-priorities-update-2026}} {{c:2026-06-02|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1773}}.
  2. Add a validation module that accepts passkeys: verify P256 onchain through the EIP-7951 precompile, and keys without an address through the ERC-7913 interface {{c:2025-12-16|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1213}}. Bear in mind that dankrad does not think a passkey amounts to a hardware wallet built into your phone, though it is handy for spinning up hot wallets {{c:2026-05-31|https://x.com/dankrad/status/2061047552363917436}}.
  3. Have an ERC-4337 paymaster sponsor gas, and let the account code handle batched calls.
  4. Receive through ERC-5564/6538 stealth addresses. kohaku-cli already bundles the transfer and its announcement into a single 4337 UserOp {{c:2026-08-10|https://x.com/kassandraETH/status/2086842348323872918}}.
  5. For recovery, Safe offers a way to keep the same Safe and rotate its owner to a quantum-safe, hash-based signer {{c:2026-04-01|https://x.com/koeppelmann/status/2039095395419148292}}, and there is a proposal for guardian-free timelock recovery for 4337/7579 accounts {{c:2026-07-14|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/timelock-account-recovery-a-trust-minimized-recovery-system-for-smart-accounts/29009}}.

Path B: the L1 target after Hegotá (settled plus candidates).

  1. Switch to EIP-8141 frame transactions: a validation frame calls the account's own validation logic, a payer frame approves the fee, and execution frames batch calls atomically {{c:2026-05-13|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11652}}.
  2. EIP-8250 keyed nonces let transactions run in parallel and let protocols such as privacy pools share one sender {{c:2026-05-12|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11598}}.
  3. EIP-8272 lets transactions reference recent state roots. soispoke argues that together with FOCIL, this set gives Ethereum native, trustless, censorship-resistant private transactions in Hegotá {{c:2026-05-26|https://x.com/soispoke/status/2059269087381852618}}.
  4. EIP-7906 assertions let a wallet show exactly what a transaction will do, and revert it if the result does not match {{c:2026-11-06|https://x.com/ethereumfndn/status/2118479704412045627|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions}}.
  5. Existing ERC-7579 modules plug into frame transactions through ERC-8286, which keeps migration costs down {{c:2026-07-28|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1794}}.

Path C: L2s first. Base plans to ship EIP-8130 {{c:2026-08-21|https://x.com/jessepollak/status/2090844327748796902}}. EIP-8430 gives existing EOAs batching, sponsorship and 2D nonces and may land on L2s first, while L1 goes with EIP-8141. The two use different transaction fields and mempool rules, so SDKs will need to support both {{c:2026-09-29|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12385}} {{c:2026-09-30|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8430-aa-transaction-type-split-out-from-8130/29810}}.

4. Trade-offs and open questions

The main trade-offs

  • A general platform or a turnkey account. lightclient argues that half of 8130 is application-layer detail that does not belong in the protocol, and that 8141 leaves room for permissionless innovation {{c:2026-08-22|https://x.com/lightclients/status/2090862398928552188}}. Ethlabs' weekly update describes 8130 as a turnkey account with strong defaults and 8141 as a platform for accounts {{c:2026-08-31}}. The cost: 8141 is flexible but leaves wallets to implement validation, while 8130 is easier to integrate but limits the patterns available {{c:2026-08-22|https://x.com/lightclients/status/2090862398928552188}}.
  • Privacy in the protocol, or primitives for apps. 8182 was declined while 8250 and 8272 became candidates (see above), so privacy features will mostly live in apps and wallets.
  • Protect the account or protect the asset. If a key can re-delegate, any policy written into the account can be swapped out, which is why some argue for putting rate limits in the asset {{c:2026-11-02|https://ethresear.ch/t/asset-level-spending-limits-and-recovery-should-rate-limiting-live-in-the-asset-rather-than-the-account/26205}}.
  • 7702's convenience versus its standing risk. One signature upgrades an EOA, and that is just as efficient for an attacker {{c:2026-10-30|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12600}}. EIP-8476 gives institutions a way to opt out of delegation for good, and EIP-8477 makes unused authorizations expire.
  • Post-quantum: switch the EOA's scheme directly, or go through a smart account. EIP-8202 lets an EOA pick its signature algorithm with a scheme_id and no smart account, a different route from 8141 and 8164 {{c:2026-04-23|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11438}}.

Still open

  • When Hegotá reaches mainnet: there is no date in the sources. Glamsterdam's fork on Hoodi was postponed over EIP-8037's compatibility with existing contracts, with no new date {{c:2026-10-22|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/22/glamsterdam-hoodi-delay}}, and ACDE #249 on November 19 will discuss the audit results {{c:2026-11-11|https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285}}.
  • The final shape of 8141: the SFI decision said the implementation and even the EIP number could change {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}}. Derek Chiang added that SFI only signals Ethereum will ship AA in Hegotá, not that the current text is fixed, and weekly calls are now trying to converge 8141 and 8130 {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}} {{c:2026-08-28|https://x.com/decentrek/status/2093026086552752141}}.
  • Whether the validation gas limit can fit zk proof verification: the privacy pool test exceeded it {{c:2026-11-06|https://ethresear.ch/t/a-privacy-pool-on-frame-transactions-what-works-and-the-case-that-does-not-yet/26223}}.
  • Whether EIP-8250 switches to single-use spent flags {{c:2026-10-09|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12467}}; EIP-8298 SETCODEFROM is still under discussion as a Hegotá candidate {{c:2026-10-07|https://ethresear.ch/t/who-cleans-up-the-code-setcodefroms-dangling-bytecode-and-futureproof-design-choices/26127}}.
  • No consensus on a post-quantum signature scheme: Falcon, ML-DSA and hash-based signatures all have drafts or implementations under discussion {{c:2026-04-11|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11511}} {{c:2026-06-05|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/post-quantum-erc-4337-wots-39-winternitz-one-time-signature-wallet-for-ethereum/28715}}.
  • Delegation safeguards: EIP-8476 and EIP-8477 are both drafts.

5. What the sources do not cover

  • ERC-6900, ERC-7715, ERC-7710 and ERC-7739 never appeared in this year's reports, so we do not assess them.
  • The sources record no status change for ERC-7579 itself, only the related ERC-7780 module types and ERC-8286.
  • There is nothing in the sources on which L2s support the P256 precompile, or on market share or audits of specific wallets.
  • The sources have no Hegotá mainnet date and no full picture of how far each client has implemented EIP-8141.
