Scope: the site's 365 daily reports from 2025-11-12 to 2026-11-11 and the original sources they cite; the current status of each standard was also checked on 2026-11-11 (UTC+7) against the ethereum/EIPs and ethereum/ERCs repositories and the Hegotá meta EIP (EIP-8081). After each claim, the brackets link first to that day's report on this site and then to the original source; links marked "checked" are original pages read directly on 2026-11-11. Status labels: live means activated on Ethereum mainnet, or a Final standard already used by wallets and contracts; Scheduled for Inclusion (SFI) means scheduled for an upgrade, final form not settled; candidate (CFI) means Considered for Inclusion, in an upgrade's scope but still subject to change; proposed means Draft, PFI, or an unmerged PR; rejected means DFI or turned down on a call; withdrawn means Withdrawn. Items marked "our recommendation" are a judgment drawn from the sources, not a decision by any team.

Key takeaways

  1. You can assemble most features today, but you must choose between keeping your address and being able to rotate the root key.EIP-7702 lets an EOA run contract code and went live with Pectra {{c:2026-02-18|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/18/protocol-priorities-update-2026}}; ERC-4337 became Final on 2026-06-02 {{c:2026-06-02|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1773}}; ERC-7913 became Final on 2025-12-16 {{c:2025-12-16|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1213}}. But after a 7702 delegation the original ECDSA key keeps full control {{c:2026-04-10|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11506}}, and EIP-7851, which would switch it off, was declined for Hegotá {{c:2026-09-15|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12335}}. So today, rotating the root key or moving to post-quantum signatures means deploying a smart account at a new address (Path A2 below).
  2. L1 native AA is Scheduled for Inclusion as EIP-8141 frame transactions, final form not settled.On 2026-08-27 EIP-8141 became SFI for Hegotá, with a note that the implementation, including the EIP number, may still change {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}}; ACDE #245 on 2026-09-10 made it Hegotá's execution-layer headliner {{c:2026-09-10|https://x.com/vdWijden/status/2098068285791121842}} {{r:https://ethereal.news/ethereal-news-weekly-39/|Ethereal news #39}}; the consensus-layer headliner is FOCIL {{c:2026-04-10|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/04/10/checkpoint-9}}. Base says it will ship EIP-8130 on L2 {{c:2026-08-21|https://x.com/jessepollak/status/2090844327748796902}}; 8130 has been narrowed to the Keystore only {{c:2026-10-08|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12386}}, and the split-out transaction type is proposed in PR #12385, still unmerged as of 2026-11-11. The number "EIP-8430" so far comes from the discussion thread and an Ethereal news roundup, not from a merged EIP {{c:2026-09-29|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12385}} {{c:2026-10-08|https://x.com/EtherealnewsHQ/status/2107920083997876441}}.
  3. Privacy follows "the protocol provides primitives, apps build privacy".EIP-8250 keyed nonces, EIP-8272 recent roots and EIP-7906 transaction assertions became Hegotá candidates (CFI) on 2026-09-24 {{c:2026-09-24|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12377}}, while EIP-8182, in-protocol private transfers, was declined at ACDE #245 and recorded in the meta EIP on 2026-09-15 {{c:2026-09-15|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12335}}.
  4. The year's biggest account security incident came down to authorization paths, not cryptography. The Binance incident on 2026-10-29 affected about $6.3B {{c:2026-10-30|https://ethereal.news/ethereal-news-weekly-45/}}. The preliminary report says that once the attacker controlled the signing service, it took 50 transactions to set up a malicious 7702 delegation, and revoking it took longer because the wallets used MPC {{c:2026-11-03|https://x.com/binance/status/2117626126214504729}}. lightclient, one of the 7702 authors, called 7702 "just an accelerant for an existing attack" {{c:2026-11-03|https://x.com/dapplion/status/2117362449942708670}}.
  5. Post-quantum support only goes as far as "swappable and aggregatable"; no scheme is chosen.EIP-8141 moved signatures into a separate list, leaving room for future aggregation including post-quantum signatures, merged 2026-05-22 {{c:2026-05-22|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11481}}; EIP-8288 (post-quantum signature and STARK aggregation frames) was merged as Draft on 2026-09-09 {{c:2026-09-09|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11772}}, and EIP-8425 (quantum freeze and account recovery) as Draft on 2026-10-24 {{c:2026-10-24|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12383}}; PR #12616, adding lean SPHINCS hash signatures to 8141, was opened on 2026-11-04 and is unmerged as of 11-11 {{c:2026-11-04|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12616}}.

Putting it together

Path A1: keep the existing EOA address (works today; our recommendation for everyday, small-balance accounts).

  1. Delegate the EOA with EIP-7702 to an audited ERC-7579 modular account implementation to get batching, sponsorship and a passkey validation module. 7702 is live and 4337 is Final {{c:2026-02-18|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/18/protocol-priorities-update-2026}} {{c:2026-06-02|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1773}}.
  2. Limit: the root key cannot be rotated. The original ECDSA key always keeps full control {{c:2026-04-10|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11506}} and can sign a new delegation that replaces any recovery or limit policy written into the account {{c:2026-11-02|https://ethresear.ch/t/asset-level-spending-limits-and-recovery-should-rate-limiting-live-in-the-asset-rather-than-the-account/26205}}. EIP-7851, which would disable the original key, was rejected {{c:2026-09-15|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12335}}.
  3. No post-quantum migration path: for an EOA the key is the account, so migration cannot just swap keys {{c:2026-01-16|https://ethresear.ch/t/migration-strategies-for-eoas-under-the-quantum-threat-breakages-and-open-questions/23864}}. EIP-8151 and EIP-8298 are Hegotá candidates; if both ship, a 7702 account could switch to regular code and the old key would stop working with ecRecover. This is our inference from the two specs, not a settled path {{c:2026-10-08|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12460}} {{r:https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8151|EIP-8151}}.
  4. Receive through ERC-5564/6538 stealth addresses; kohaku-cli has shown a transfer and its announcement bundled into one 4337 UserOp {{c:2026-08-10|https://x.com/kassandraETH/status/2086842348323872918}}.

Path A2: deploy a smart account at a new address (works today; our recommendation for large or long-lived accounts).

  1. Deploy an ERC-4337 compatible smart account (for example an ERC-7579 modular account or a Safe). Its address differs from the old EOA, so assets have to move.
  2. The validation module accepts passkeys: P256 is checked on chain through the EIP-7951 precompile, non-address keys through the ERC-7913 interface {{c:2025-12-16|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1213}}, and the account presents contract signatures to apps through ERC-1271 {{r:https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1271|ERC-1271}}. dankrad argues, though, that a passkey is not the same as a hardware wallet built into the phone; it mainly suits quickly created hot wallets {{c:2026-05-31|https://x.com/dankrad/status/2061047552363917436}}.
  3. An ERC-4337 paymaster sponsors gas, account code runs batches, and wallets can start them with EIP-5792 wallet_sendCalls {{r:https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5792|EIP-5792}}.
  4. Rotation and recovery: Safe already offers a path that keeps the Safe address and rotates owners to quantum-resistant hash-based signers {{c:2026-04-01|https://x.com/koeppelmann/status/2039095395419148292}}; there is also discussion of guardian-free timelock recovery for 4337/7579 accounts {{c:2026-07-14|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/timelock-account-recovery-a-trust-minimized-recovery-system-for-smart-accounts/29009}}; post-quantum signatures can be tried inside 4337 contract accounts first, such as the WOTS-39 wallet based on Winternitz one-time signatures {{c:2026-06-05|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/post-quantum-erc-4337-wots-39-winternitz-one-time-signature-wallet-for-ethereum/28715}}.

Path B: the L1 target after Hegotá (Scheduled for Inclusion plus candidates, final form not settled).

  1. Switch to EIP-8141 frame transactions: a validation frame calls the account's own validation logic, a payer frame approves the fee, and execution frames batch calls atomically {{c:2026-05-13|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11652}}.
  2. EIP-8250 keyed nonces let transactions run in parallel and let protocols such as privacy pools share one sender {{c:2026-05-12|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11598}}.
  3. EIP-8272 lets transactions reference recent state roots. soispoke argues that together with FOCIL, this set gives Ethereum native, trustless, censorship-resistant private transactions in Hegotá {{c:2026-05-26|https://x.com/soispoke/status/2059269087381852618}}.
  4. EIP-7906 assertions let a wallet show exactly what a transaction will do, and revert it if the result does not match {{c:2026-11-06|https://x.com/ethereumfndn/status/2118479704412045627|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions}}.
  5. Existing ERC-7579 modules plug into frame transactions through ERC-8286, which keeps migration costs down {{c:2026-07-28|https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1794}}.

Path C: L2 first.Base plans to ship EIP-8130 {{c:2026-08-21|https://x.com/jessepollak/status/2090844327748796902}}; the transaction type split from 8130 gives existing EOAs batching, sponsorship and 2D nonces and may land on L2 first, while L1 goes with EIP-8141. Their transaction fields and mempool rules differ, so SDKs need to support both {{c:2026-09-29|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12385}} {{c:2026-09-30|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8430-aa-transaction-type-split-out-from-8130/29810}}. That transaction type's PR is still unmerged as of 2026-11-11.

4. Trade-offs and open questions

The main trade-offs

  • A general platform or a turnkey account. lightclient argues that half of 8130 is application-layer detail that does not belong in the protocol, and that 8141 leaves room for permissionless innovation {{c:2026-08-22|https://x.com/lightclients/status/2090862398928552188}}. Ethlabs' weekly update describes 8130 as a turnkey account with strong defaults and 8141 as a platform for accounts {{c:2026-08-31}}. The cost: 8141 is flexible but leaves wallets to implement validation, while 8130 is easier to integrate but limits the patterns available {{c:2026-08-22|https://x.com/lightclients/status/2090862398928552188}}.
  • Privacy in the protocol, or primitives for apps. 8182 was declined while 8250 and 8272 became candidates (see above), so privacy features will mostly live in apps and wallets.
  • Protect the account or protect the asset. If a key can re-delegate, any policy written into the account can be swapped out, which is why some argue for putting rate limits in the asset {{c:2026-11-02|https://ethresear.ch/t/asset-level-spending-limits-and-recovery-should-rate-limiting-live-in-the-asset-rather-than-the-account/26205}}.
  • 7702's convenience versus its standing risk. One signature upgrades an EOA, and that is just as efficient for an attacker {{c:2026-10-30|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12600}}. EIP-8476 gives institutions a way to opt out of delegation for good, and EIP-8477 makes unused authorizations expire.
  • Post-quantum: switch the EOA's scheme directly, or go through a smart account. EIP-8202 lets an EOA pick its signature algorithm with a scheme_id and no smart account, a different route from 8141 and 8164 {{c:2026-04-23|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11438}}.

Still open

  • Hegotá mainnet timing: the sources give no date; Ethereal news #39 says it targets 2027 {{r:https://ethereal.news/ethereal-news-weekly-39/|Ethereal news #39}}. Glamsterdam's Hoodi fork was postponed over EIP-8037 contract compatibility, with no new date {{c:2026-10-22|https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/22/glamsterdam-hoodi-delay}}, and ACDE #249 on November 19 will discuss the audit results {{c:2026-11-11|https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285}}.
  • The final shape of 8141: the SFI decision said the implementation and even the EIP number could change {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}}. Derek Chiang added that SFI only signals Ethereum will ship AA in Hegotá, not that the current text is fixed, and weekly calls are now trying to converge 8141 and 8130 {{c:2026-08-27|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12251}} {{c:2026-08-28|https://x.com/decentrek/status/2093026086552752141}}.
  • Validation gas limit: Nethermind found 300k workable with a client-side protection {{c:2026-11-07|https://ethresear.ch/t/how-large-can-eip-8141s-validation-budget-be-a-mempool-dos-benchmark/26224}}; PR #12644 proposing 300,000 is unmerged {{c:2026-11-10|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644}}, as is PR #12617, which leaves signature calldata cost out of MAX_VERIFY_GAS {{c:2026-11-04|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12617}}.
  • EIP-8298 SETCODEFROM is a Hegotá candidate (CFI) {{c:2026-10-08|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12460}}, but design questions such as cleaning up dangling bytecode are still being discussed {{c:2026-10-07|https://ethresear.ch/t/who-cleans-up-the-code-setcodefroms-dangling-bytecode-and-futureproof-design-choices/26127}}.
  • No agreed post-quantum signature scheme: Falcon, ML-DSA and hash-based signatures all have drafts or implementation discussions {{c:2026-04-11|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11511}} {{c:2026-06-05|https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/post-quantum-erc-4337-wots-39-winternitz-one-time-signature-wallet-for-ethereum/28715}}; PR #12616, adding lean SPHINCS to 8141, is unmerged {{c:2026-11-04|https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12616}}.
  • Delegation safeguards: EIP-8476 (PR #12600) and EIP-8477 (PR #12601) are both unmerged proposals.

5. What the sources do not cover

  • ERC-6900, ERC-7715, ERC-7710 and ERC-7739 never appeared in this year's reports, so we do not assess them.
  • ERC-7579 is still Draft in the repository; the sources only record the related ERC-7780 module types and ERC-8286 {{r:https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7579|ERC-7579}}.
  • There is nothing in the sources on which L2s support the P256 precompile, or on market share or audits of specific wallets.
  • The sources have no Hegotá mainnet date and no full picture of how far each client has implemented EIP-8141.
