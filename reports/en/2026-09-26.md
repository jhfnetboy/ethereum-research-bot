## Key topics

### 1. Lighter nodes
**What happened**
- [Vitalik: you can now sync an Ethereum node within half a day, and with aggressive settings it takes under half a terabyte of disk](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2103636111260373176), 07:01, thanks to EIP-4444 and client teams' snap sync work, with Glamsterdam improving sync further.
- [nero_eth: Glamsterdam takes snap sync to the next level](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2103654571398877443), 08:15, with snap v2 using the block-level access list, a state diff, to replace the traditional healing phase; [nero_eth: EIP-7928 is approaching the final mile](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2103806304469008385), 18:18, with significant throughput gains from parallelism and compatibility with zkEVMs, partially stateful nodes and inclusion lists.
- [Vitalik: PeerDAS running for nearly a year with basically no problems is an underappreciated triumph of client developers](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2103825405530853710), 19:33.

**Why it matters**
- The easier nodes are to run, the more users can verify the chain themselves and the less they depend on centralized RPCs, which also bears on privacy.

### 2. Account authorization
**What happened**
- [Magicians: ERC-8427 portable spend grants](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8427-portable-spend-grants/29776), 00:26 (tankcdr), where a principal registers a multi-asset spend grant with per-call, trailing-window and lifetime caps in an immutable registry and the delegate holds only the grant's id; it requires ERC-1271, EIP-7702 and others, with an [ERC PR](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2037).

**Why it matters**
- For wallets and agents, giving an AI agent or a subscription service capped spending authority is one of the most direct use cases for account abstraction.
