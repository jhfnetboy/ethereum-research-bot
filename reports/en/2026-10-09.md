## Highlights
1. **Assertion semantics**: EIP-7906 merged "a failed assertion reverts the execution body" and specified how TXDIFF and POST_TX rollback are recorded in the BAL.
2. **Frame transaction structure**: EIP-8141 defines the validation prefix and execution body under Behavior, and EIP-8077 defines source and nonce for frame transactions.
3. **Keyed nonces**: an EIP-8250 PR proposes single-use spent flags for keyed nonces, and an EIP-8130 PR simplifies the Keystore.
4. **ERC-8441**: stealth addresses that combine ML-KEM-768 with secp256k1 ECDH. Spending is unchanged, and the deployed ERC-5564 and ERC-6538 contracts are reused.

## Key topics

### 1. Frame transactions and assertions: execution semantics settle
**What happened**
- [EIP-8141: define the validation prefix and execution body under Behavior](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12471) was merged, [along with naming the TXPARAM, FRAMEPARAM and SIGPARAM params](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12469) (spencer-tb).
- EIP-7906: [a failed assertion reverts the execution body](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12466) (danceratopz) and [BAL recording for TXDIFF and POST_TX rollback](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12463) (forshtat) were both merged; [stating the POST_TX rollback point via the EIP-8141 validation prefix](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12472) was submitted.
- [EIP-8250: make keyed nonces single-use spent flags](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12467) was submitted (nerolation); [EIP-8130: simplify the Keystore](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12462) was submitted (chunter-cb).
- [EIP-8077: define source and nonce for frame transactions](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12139) was merged; this is the networking EIP for announcing transactions with their nonce.

**Why it matters**
- Separating the validation prefix from the execution body means failures in validation (signature, payment) and in execution can be handled separately: a failed assertion reverts only the execution body and leaves the validation phase intact. As single-use flags, keyed nonces look more like the nullifiers in privacy pools, and the storage cost of such flags will be affected by Glamsterdam's state pricing.

### 2. Privacy: post-quantum stealth addresses and a private stablecoin
**What happened**
- [Magicians: ERC-8441 hybrid post-quantum stealth address scheme](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8441-hybrid-post-quantum-stealth-address-scheme/29923), 11:18 (namncc, [PR](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2059)). It defines schemeId 3 under ERC-5564: the payment secret combines an ML-KEM-768 encapsulation with a secp256k1 ECDH secret, so as long as ML-KEM-768 holds, an adversary with a future quantum computer still cannot tell from the announcement log who was paid. Each stealth address is still an ordinary secp256k1 EOA, with no protocol change and no new contract.
- [ethresear.ch: a private stablecoin on Ethereum](https://ethresear.ch/t/private-stablecoin-on-ethereum/26140), 19:40, backed by USDC/USDT, with minting and redemption enforced by contracts and transactions kept confidential or unlinkable to identities.
- [Aztec: Nyx lets you send and receive privately in Rabby and earn on Aave with no link back to you](https://x.com/aztecnetwork/status/2108258672745070621), 01:10; [Aztec: private inference covers three privacy layers, payment, identity and words](https://x.com/aztecnetwork/status/2108558518534177111), 21:01.

**Why it matters**
- ERC-8441 protects only who received a payment against future quantum hindsight; spending still relies on secp256k1. It addresses the "harvest now, decrypt later" privacy risk, not fund security, and wallets can adopt it today.
