## Key topics

### 1. Account abstraction: ERC-4337 reaches Final
**What happened**
- [ERC-4337: move to Final](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1773) (yoavw), merged; the PR was submitted on May 26.
- [ERC-8126: move to Final](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1769) (Cybercentry), merged.

**Why it matters**
- For accounts: from its 2021 proposal to Final, ERC-4337's EntryPoint, bundler and paymaster interfaces are now fixed, so wallets and infrastructure can rely on them long term; native AA proposals such as EIP-8141, EIP-8130 and EIP-8250 are moving the same capabilities into the protocol, and a final 4337 becomes the stable reference between the two.

**Why it matters**
- For consensus: registering post-quantum public keys onchain first lets validators switch signature schemes later, instead of rushing a migration when the threat is close.

### 3. Privacy
**What happened**
- [aztecnetwork: every privacy network claims selective disclosure but does not say who holds the keys; Canton, Tempo, zkSync and Starknet put control with an operator, a node or a standing key, while on Aztec control stays with the user](https://x.com/aztecnetwork/status/2061843589462913103), 23:13.
- [AndyGuzmanEth: we really need CROPS stablecoins; compliant tokens plus privacy protocols are not enough, and compliance is not censorship resistance](https://x.com/AndyGuzmanEth/status/2061512854927257960), 01:19.
- [pcaversaccio: Circle unblocklisted Zama after Zama upgraded the cUSDC implementation contract](https://x.com/pcaversaccio/status/2061567394716528959), 04:55.

**Why it matters**
- For privacy: selective disclosure is only worth as much as control over who discloses; if an operator holds the viewing keys, privacy reduces to trusting that operator.
