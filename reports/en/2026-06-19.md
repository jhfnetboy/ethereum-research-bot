## Key topics

### 1. Repricing and scaling in Hegotá
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: Scaling in Hegota, using the ETH transfer to anchor execution and bandwidth](https://ethresear.ch/t/scaling-in-hegota-using-the-eth-transfer-to-anchor-execution-and-bandwidth/25232), 15:19 (misilva73). The starting point is that the 21,000-gas ETH transfer bounds both dimensions of the slot: on the execution side, the Glamsterdam repricings pushed costs as far as the transfer cap allows; on the bandwidth side, a block full of transfers has an irreducible byte density. The post works out what Hegotá must change to keep raising the gas limit.
- [EIP-8007: refresh repricing summaries for 2780, 7904, 8037, 8038](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11817) (misilva73), submitted and merged.
- [EIP-8037: explain the 101 byte count in `REGULAR_PER_AUTH_BASE_COST`](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11818) (danceratopz), submitted; the number raised questions during the 8038 EELS implementation.

**Why it matters**
- For users: the transfer price anchors gas pricing, and if Hegotá raises throughput without changing 21,000, other resources such as calldata must be repriced (the next day misilva73 submitted an EIP raising the calldata floor to 96). The base cost per 7702 authorization is derived from its byte size, which affects fees for transactions with authorizations.

### 2. FOCIL and L2 forced transactions
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: Repurposing FOCIL as an L2 forced transaction mechanism](https://ethresear.ch/t/repurposing-focil-as-an-l2-forced-transaction-mechanism/25233), 16:39 (donnoh), touching FOCIL (SFI), optional execution proofs (PFI), BALs (SFI), validity-only partial statelessness and native rollups.

**Why it matters**
- For L2 users: forced transactions are the escape hatch when a sequencer censors, and reusing L1 inclusion lists would let L2s inherit L1 censorship resistance.
