## Key topics

### 1. Transaction floor and block size: EIP-7702 authorizations join the floor
**What happened**
- [PR #12680: align EIP-8131 with Glamsterdam](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12680) was merged on 2026-11-21 at 15:48 (author Toni Wahrstätter). [EIP-8131](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-8131.md) (Unified Transaction Content Floor) charges every user-controlled byte in a transaction (calldata, access lists, EIP-7702 authorizations, EIP-4844 blob hashes) a flat 64 gas at the floor. Until now 7702 authorization tuples and blob hashes paid nothing at the floor. This update:
  - Prices authorizations at 133 bytes, their largest valid encoding. The PR notes that at 108 bytes a block made entirely of authorizations could exceed the bound.
  - Rebases the floor on the EIP-2780 base cost instead of 21,000, and keeps the EIP-7981 surcharge in intrinsic gas.
  - Restates the block size bound through the EIP-8037 execution dimension, in payload bytes.
  - Adds a new impact report covering 90 days and all 168M mainnet transactions: against Glamsterdam, 0.66% of transactions pay more (+0.09% gas overall), and all of them are blob or type-4 (EIP-7702) transactions.
- [PR #12681: align EIP-8279 with Glamsterdam](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12681) was merged on 2026-11-21 at 16:01. [EIP-8279](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-8279.md) (Block Access List Byte Floor) meters the bytes execution adds to the EIP-7928 BAL at runtime and folds them into the EIP-8131 floor at 64 gas per byte. This update:
  - Recomputes the execution cost table for the new EIP-2780, EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 costs, giving a per-authorization floor of 11,776 gas. Under the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap, the cold SLOAD bypass can pack 1.546 MiB into a block.
  - Splits the block size bound into metered payload (at most gas_limit/64) and fixed per-transaction overhead, with numbers for the delegated-target case.
  - Adds an impact report from 400 traced blocks: at Glamsterdam execution costs, runtime metering makes at most about 3.7% of transactions more expensive (+0.83% gas), versus 17% at today's costs.
- The same day at 18:04, [PR #12581](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12581) (EIP-2780: credit state gas refills to the reservoir and add a legacy share), [PR #12582](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12582) (EIP-2780: state gas allowance for call frames) and [PR #12588](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12588) (EIP-8141: charge the EIP-8037 legacy share as extra execution gas) were closed by their author without merging. In the #12581 discussion, gretzke had used the Uniswap v4 PositionManager's `notifyUnsubscribe{gas: 150,000}` to show that whether the same call runs out of gas depends on the tx.gas the user signs.

**Why it matters**
- EIP-7702 lets a regular account (EOA) delegate to contract code, and the authorization tuple is the entry point for that upgrade. Under EIP-8131, authorizations pay a per-byte floor, so transactions with many authorizations pay slightly more, while over 99% of ordinary transactions are unaffected.
- Together the two EIPs cap the worst-case size of a 60M gas block at about 0.89 MiB (gas_limit/64), one of the preconditions for raising the gas limit further.
- With the three state gas PRs closed, Glamsterdam's EIP-8037 metering is set to ship as is. Contracts and wallets with hard-coded gas budgets have to adapt themselves rather than wait for a protocol fix.

**What to watch**
- EIP-8131 and EIP-8279 are both still Draft, and inclusion in an upgrade depends on ACD discussion. [ACDE #250](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/all-core-devs-execution-acde-250-december-3-2026/30463) is set for December 3 at 21:00 with Glamsterdam, Hegotá and misc on the agenda.
- Wallets and relayers doing batched 7702 authorizations or sponsored transactions: budget the floor at 133 bytes per authorization.

### 2. PQ account signatures: signature lifetime, key rotation and the "pqai" label
**What happened**
- [EIPsInsight posted extra PQTS #17 takeaways on Magicians](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/post-quantum-transaction-signature-pqts-breakout-17/30058/5) on 2026-11-21 at 23:23 (the call itself was on October 28; [full notes](https://eipsinsight.com/calls/pqts/017)):
  - Key rotation: five major exchanges said automated key rotation is fairly easy. At a lifetime of 2^24 signatures, Circle's volume would mean rotating roughly every 1.5 years.
  - Hardware wallets: Project 11 pointed to significant performance limits on Ledger at higher signature lifetimes.
  - Privacy: pruning speeds up key generation but brings privacy trade-offs after about 75 signatures.
  - Forest-of-WOTS signs over 30% faster but raises MPC compatibility and security analysis challenges, and SNARK-based proof-of-grinding could eventually give a 10x improvement.
- [PR #12672 (leanSPHINCS dependency frames)](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12672) took an editorial change suggested by abcoathup on 2026-11-21 at 17:36. On 11-20 it had already filled in the dependency verification frame encoding and the leanSPHINCS scheme definition (the 32-byte public key, 32-byte message and leansphincs_verify interface are fixed, other parameters are provisional). It is still a draft waiting for an EIP number.
- On X, [gballet](https://x.com/gballet/status/2124054653151092846) argued on 2026-11-21 at 15:17 that "pqai" is a dangerous label: quantum security does not imply AI security, and the two may involve a trade-off. [donnoh](https://x.com/donnoh_eth/status/2124059038564630744) agreed at 15:35 and said he is writing a post. [Dankrad](https://x.com/dankrad/status/2124069866147848647) replied at 16:18 that a very conservative PQ scheme is also more likely to survive AI cryptanalysis, and that schemes can be stacked, for example SPHINCS plus a required EC signature, which strictly increases security. He [added at 16:43](https://x.com/dankrad/status/2124076187806765076) that nothing can be proven unbreakable, hash-based schemes still rely on a specific hash function, and adding an EC signature costs almost nothing. [barnabé](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2124170965575971042) said at 22:59 that pqai does not rule out a dual-signature scheme.

**Why it matters**
- Signature lifetime decides when a PQ account must change keys. Shorter lifetimes mean smaller signatures and faster hardware wallets, but high-volume signers such as exchanges and payment firms rotate more often. That ends up in wallet and custody operations.
- If "PQ signature plus EC signature" becomes standard practice, account verification code and the VERIFY gas budget need room for two signatures, which ties directly into last issue's discussion of raising MAX_VERIFY_GAS to 300k.

**What to watch**
- Whether a default lifetime (2^24 or higher) is settled, when PR #12672 gets a number, and whether Iván proposes it for Hegotá at the next ACDE.
- Wallet teams: measure signing time on hardware devices at different lifetimes, and think about the UX of automated rotation.

### 3. Agent accounts and privacy: readable policies, measurable linkability
**What happened**
- [Draft ERC: Policy-Enforced Agent Accounts](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/draft-erc-policy-enforced-agent-accounts/30467) was opened by palisec-dev on 2026-11-21 at 23:07, with [ERCs PR #2196](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2196). It only defines a read and evidence layer, not how enforcement works:
  - Discovery (ERC-165): policySigner, policyOf, pendingPolicyOf, mandateOf and isStopped, served by the account itself or by a singleton registry for existing accounts such as Safes.
  - Policy commitments: the keccak256 hash of the policy document's RFC 8785 canonical JSON, with an optional URI so anyone can check it.
  - Pending loosenings are public with an effectiveAt so watchers have time to react, refusal reasons use an append-only bytes32 list, and decision receipts are EIP-712 signatures bound to the chain, account, target, value, calldata hash and policy hash, with ERC-1271 for contract signers.
  - The authors see it as complementary to ERC-7579 and ERC-7780 (enforcement inside the account), ERC-7710 (delegation with caveats) and ERC-8226. A reference registry is deployed on Robinhood Chain mainnet (4663).
- [Zealynx's review](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/draft-erc-policy-enforced-agent-accounts/30467/3) on 2026-11-22 at 06:08 pointed out that activate accepts any higher version and applies it immediately, and since the registry only sees a hash it cannot tell a tightening from a loosening, so the delay is only as good as the publisher. Receipt verification does not check the current policy version or isStopped and does not remember decisionId, so a receipt can be reused. For Safes, call and delegatecall produce the same intentHash, so the intent should include the operation or the ERC-7579 execution mode.
- [Pre-ERC: Agent Linkability Report](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/pre-erc-agent-linkability-report/30462) on 2026-11-21 at 15:03 ([ERCs PR #2193](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2193)) proposes a report format describing how far public chain data can link an ERC-8004 agent's owner, agentWallet and payment address. Every field either carries evidence or states why it was not read. Reading the Robinhood Chain identity registry on 11-21, the author found that 25,620 of 26,235 agents have no agentWallet separate from the owner, so payments land on the owner's own address.
- The FHE privacy debate: after the official Ethereum account published Zama's guest thread last issue, the discussion around Zama continued. [pcaversaccio](https://x.com/pcaversaccio/status/2124077434697846817) criticised it on 2026-11-21 at 16:48 as "vendor-dependent privacy", since the code licence grants no patent rights and commercial deployments need Zama's permission. [donnoh](https://x.com/donnoh_eth/status/2124153379895267352) said at 21:50 that in many cases exact amounts or tight bounds can be derived with linear math, and later transactions can leak privacy retroactively.

**Why it matters**
- More and more funds are moved by agent accounts. Whether outsiders can read and verify a policy decides whether lenders and wallets can trust an agent account's limits, and the review shows a public delay only means something if it also binds the publisher.
- A linkability report turns privacy leaks into comparable data, so wallets can nudge users to give agents a separate payment address or a stealth address (ERC-6538/5564).

**What to watch**
- How the PR #2196 authors respond to Zealynx (a minimum delay, receipt replay protection, the Safe operation type).
- Developers taking payments through agents: at minimum keep agentWallet separate from the owner address.
