## Key topics

### 1. Frame transaction family fixes merged
**What happened**
- EIP-8141 merged: [pin the SECP256K1 signature encoding](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11937), [execution approval authorizes all subsequent sender frames](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11939), [stack operand order for FRAMEDATALOAD/FRAMEDATACOPY](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11938), and [apply the EIP-7623 calldata floor to frame and signature data](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11941).
- EIP-8272 merged: [restore 8141 signature costs in tx_gas_limit](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11931), [fix the TXPARAM constant](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11930), [restore the signatures field and elide signature bytes in the signature hash](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11959), and [strengthen mempool handling of recent root references](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11961).
- EIP-8250: [align NONCE_MANAGER activation wording with 8272](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11960), merged; [restore signatures in the payload and correct the signature-hash description](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11963) (AnkushinDaniil), submitted and merged.

**Why it matters**
- For wallets and clients: once signature encoding, the signature hash and the calldata floor are pinned, different implementations can sign and charge frame transactions consistently, a step in converging the AA spec before devnet-8.

### 2. EIP-7716 anti-correlation penalties
**What happened**
- [EIP-7716: move to Draft](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11962) (OisinKyne), submitted. EIP-7716 is a Hegotá PFI; this revision replaces the `NET_EXCESS_PENALTIES` penalty-factor mechanism with an excess-over-moving-average design: one new `Gwei` state field, `PENALTY_SLOPE = 3 · (MAX_PENALTY_FACTOR − 1)` so the cap binds at exactly one third of stake, and scaling narrowed to the target penalty of validators that missed both source and target.

**Why it matters**
- For staking operators: anti-correlation penalties hit large operators that go offline together harder, encouraging decentralized clients and infrastructure.
