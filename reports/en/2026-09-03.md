## Key topics

### 1. The validation budget for privacy apps in the public mempool
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: EIP-8141 and the minimum required validation budget for privacy applications](https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8141-and-minimum-required-validation-budget-for-privacy-applications/25889), 06:14 (mmjahanara). Privacy apps could use frame transactions to drop offchain relayers and gain strong censorship resistance, but only if their transactions can propagate through the public mempool, whose validation budget is capped at `MAX_VERIFY_GAS = 100k`. Measuring Groth16 verification, a single-note spend needs at least 211,828 gas and the largest deployed one (spending and nullifying eight notes) at least 351,828 gas; the author recommends raising the cap to at least 250k. The cap counts proof verification execution, the signature validation intrinsic cost and EIP-8250's keyed nonce first-use charge.
- [ethresear.ch: how Hegotá can influence the state roadmap](https://ethresear.ch/t/how-hegota-can-influence-the-state-roadmap/25895), 16:46 (misilva73 and CPerezz), arguing Hegotá should keep state growth within a clear budget, keep the new state created by frame transactions bounded, and defer changes whose benefit is small today but would raise the cost of the PBT migration.

**Why it matters**
- If privacy pools can only submit through relayers, there is a censorship point; the validation budget is a key parameter for whether frame transactions can truly serve privacy apps. Privacy protocol teams can use this benchmark to assess their own verification costs.

### 2. Authority management for native AA accounts
**What happened**
- [Magicians: ERC-8403 account authority lifecycle](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8403-account-authority-lifecycle/29570), 03:10 (AnkushinDaniil). It defines how an authority on a native AA account (a signing key, or a predicate such as a lock or a limit) is added, rotated and revoked. Each authority is a leaf in a committed tree whose root the account publishes; a transaction carries a membership proof, and the account verifies it in its own authorization step and withholds authorization on failure.
- [EIP-8130: optional checkpointed keystore](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12293) (pedrouid). It adds a permanent checkpointed keystore mode for non-admin actors, while admins remain onchain and independently co-authorize successor states, so a checkpointer cannot grant authority on its own; one root approval can cover a batch of keys with different authenticators, expiries and policies and be reused across transactions.
- [EIP-7906: reference the frame-family registry for opcode bytes](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12292), merged.

**Why it matters**
- With native AA, "who can act for this account" is no longer a single private key. Rotation and revocation of authorities need a shared format so wallets and audit tools can read them.
