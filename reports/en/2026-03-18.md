## Key topics

### 1. Privacy and transactions: LUCID and encrypted frame transactions
**What happened**
- [Add EIP: LUCID encrypted mempool](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11376) (anderselowsson), merged at 21:12 after being submitted on March 4; the [Magicians thread for EIP-8184](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8184-lucid-encrypted-mempool/28017) opened at 20:42.
- [ethresear.ch: Encrypted frame transactions](https://ethresear.ch/t/encrypted-frame-transactions/24440) (soispoke), 20:05: the builder commits to the full ordered transaction set before any decryption key is revealed, then executes that committed order in the same slot after reveal, so same slot encrypted execution works without splitting the block into encrypted and plaintext sections; the design reuses LUCID and EIP-8141; [author thread](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2034255732141342870), 20:08.

**Why it matters**
- For transactions: with target, calldata and amounts hidden until ordering is locked, frontrunning and sandwiching lose their information edge; building encryption into frame transactions means native AA accounts can use the encrypted mempool directly.

### 2. Gas: EIP-8037 and EIP-7928
**What happened**
- [EIP-8037: clarify regular gas must be charged before state gas](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11421) (spencer-tb), submitted: following the gas repricing call, when an opcode needs both regular and state gas the regular charge comes first, and if it runs out of gas no state gas is consumed, which keeps the parent's reservoir from inflating when a child frame fails.
- [EIP-7928: fix a contradicting claim about the fee recipient](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11420) (nerolation), submitted.

**Why it matters**
- For gas: state gas is central to the Glamsterdam repricing, and the charge order sets the cost boundary of failed calls, so clients must agree exactly.

### 3. Consensus and post-quantum
**What happened**
- [VitalikButerin: a new fast confirmation rule gives a hard no revert guarantee after one slot (12 seconds)](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2034066521937326556), 07:37, assuming an honest supermajority and network latency under about 3 seconds, one step below economic finality; [VitalikButerin: recommending a post on why dynamically available consensus matters](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2034067492595790149), 07:40.
- [ethresear.ch: What if post-quantum Ethereum doesn't need signatures at all?](https://ethresear.ch/t/what-if-post-quantum-ethereum-doesn-t-need-signatures-at-all/24427), 06:09: prove authorization semantics directly in ZK, about 4,024 R1CS constraints, 128 byte proofs and 52 ms proving, deployable today as an AA validator module with no protocol change.
- [ethresear.ch: Revisiting Falcon signature aggregation for PQ mempools](https://ethresear.ch/t/revisiting-falcon-signature-aggregation-for-pq-mempools/24431) (asanso, soispoke, b-wagn), 15:49, comparing signature and aggregate sizes under different key recovery and aggregation assumptions; [tcoratger's thread](https://x.com/tcoratger/status/2034211598919770596), 17:13.

**Why it matters**
- For accounts: post-quantum signatures are kilobytes in size and hit transactions and mempool bandwidth first; moving verification into AA modules or aggregating are paths that help without a protocol change.
