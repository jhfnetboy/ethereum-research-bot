## Key topics

### 1. EF restructuring and budget
**What happened**
- [EF blog: The EF's new structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure). The EF concluded a months-long reorganization as part of implementing the Mandate and the Treasury Management Policy. It now has five domain clusters (protocol, access, user, community and institutional layers) plus an operations cluster and a management cluster, with 54 fewer colleagues, about 20% of the EF. The protocol cluster hardens and scales the protocol, treating censorship and capture resistance, open source, privacy and security as non-negotiable protocol guarantees, including defending against toxic MEV and privileged orderflow and turning long-horizon research such as post-quantum, zkEVM and L1 privacy into protocol changes. The access layer makes CROPS properties real for individuals, and the agents acting for them, when reading the chain, transacting, proving, delegating and exiting.
- [Vitalik: the EF is cutting its budget by roughly 40% this year, which entails some difficult decisions](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2069431500035023121), 21:44, as it transitions to a long-term, endowment-based organization. Per Ethereal news #29's summary, the cuts touch clients (the multi-client model shifting from redundancy to specialization, exploring AI-assisted formal verification), PSE (Privacy Stewards of Ethereum winding down, focusing on implementing rather than exploring ZKP privacy and scaling), Devcon (smaller and more spartan) and more.
- [tkstanczak: strategically, this list is good](https://x.com/tkstanczak/status/2069454499509596582), 23:16.

**Why it matters**
- For privacy infrastructure: PSE winding down means privacy work inside the EF moves from exploration to implementation, while the protocol cluster lists L1 privacy as a protocol guarantee, shifting the focus of privacy toward the protocol and access layers.

### 2. Ethlabs founded
**What happened**
- [ethlabs_org: announcing Ethlabs, a non-profit R&D lab for Ethereum and ETH](https://x.com/ethlabs_org/status/2069104073245159573), 00:03. According to the [Thesis page on the Ethlabs website](https://ethlabs.org/thesis.html), its mission is to make Ethereum the settlement layer of the global economy; the team works with users, applications, wallets, L2s, infrastructure teams, institutions, ETH holders, core devs and researchers to turn real needs into protocol work, shared standards, infrastructure and shipped products, and describes itself as one node in a "multi-node future".
- [casparschwa: very excited to start Ethlabs](https://x.com/casparschwa/status/2069366248228495844), 17:25; Ethereum's ecosystem is accelerating its decentralization, which, like client diversity, costs more and adds coordination friction but is the principled and resilient path.

### 3. Privacy and staking
**What happened**
- [New EIP: Lean Staking](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11500) (dmpierre), merged; this is EIP-8222. Per Ethereal news #26, it was presented at ACDE #238 as staking deposits and withdrawals through a privacy pool.
- [ethbeat: STRK-20 on Starknet added to our privacy dashboard](https://x.com/ethbeat_org/status/2069424716822032524), 21:17, describing it as a centralized privacy pool with trust assumptions comparable to recent institutional privacy offerings: a single admin account has visibility over all shielded transfers.
- [aztecnetwork: Aztec reached Stage 2 on L2BEAT](https://x.com/aztecnetwork/status/2069142485562175575), 02:36; onchain governance revoked ownership of the rollup contract, the code is immutable, no admin can override the protocol, and users can always exit via the escape hatch.

**Why it matters**
- For privacy users: things called privacy pools can have very different trust models. A design where an admin sees every transfer is private to outside observers but not to its operator. If Lean Staking ships, the link between a validator's deposit and withdrawal can be broken, a protocol-level answer for staker privacy.
