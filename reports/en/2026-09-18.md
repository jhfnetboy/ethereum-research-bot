## Key topics

### 1. State design for accounts and nonces
**What happened**
- [EIP-8250: include nonce type to distinguish binary nonces](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12352) (GottfriedHerold, later closed). The background is the September 8 Bloom filter post, which suggested tagging the type in the nonce key's first byte; this PR uses a separate field instead.
- [EIP-8298: allow SETCODEFROM in initcode and require an existing source](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12356) (CPerezz, merged on the 24th). The author corrected on Magicians his own earlier review comment that had led to the initcode ban.
- [EIP-8037: remove stale EIP-7610 collision rules](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12296) was merged today; [EIP-8038: use execution-gas wording and fix the CALLCODE creation cell](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12353) was merged.

**Why it matters**
- Privacy apps use one-shot nonces as nullifiers, and tagging them separately allows more storage-efficient structures, ultimately lowering gas for private transactions. SETCODEFROM reuses code already onchain at deployment time, directly affecting smart account deployment costs.

### 2. Encrypted mempools
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: towards encrypted mempools from threshold IBE without batching](https://ethresear.ch/t/towards-encrypted-mempools-from-threshold-ibe-without-batching/26040), 23:09 (GottfriedHerold). Inspired by LUCID, a FOCIL-like ticket lands onchain first and pins execution to a known future block; only then does the sender encrypt toward the hash of the ticket-carrying block as the identity. Batch decryption is not required, which widens the choice of threshold schemes.

**Why it matters**
- Encrypted mempools prevent front-running and censorship and are part of transaction-layer privacy; relaxing the requirements on threshold schemes makes them easier to deploy.
