# Ethereum Daily 2026-11-15

Keywords: EIP-8500, post-quantum proof batching, EIP-8151, ERC-8499, EIP-8037/8038 audits

## Highlights
1. Post-quantum gas: the proof batching draft was renamed "Batched Generic Proof Dependencies" and editor abcoathup assigned it EIP-8500. The author published measurements showing a worst-case block (16,384 dependencies) verifies in just 22.5 ms on full-node-class hardware, and argues that caps, not high gas, should bound proving work.

3. Wallets and mnemonics: the ERC-8499 thread suggests computing the mnemonic fingerprint from a public key so it can't be used to test passphrase guesses offline, and picking stateful key leaves at random rather than with a counter.

## Key topics

### 1. Post-quantum transaction costs: EIP-8500 benchmarks and proposed cuts
**What happened**
- [EIP-8500 draft: Batched Generic Proof Dependencies](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12664) Between 14:22 and 22:35 on 2026-11-14, author Marchhill pushed 7 revisions, including "simplify batching to ordinary generic dependencies", "separate mempool verification budgets from gas calibration" and "bound block dependencies independently of the gas limit". At 06:17 on 2026-11-15, editor abcoathup suggested eip: 8500 in a review, assigning the official number.
- The author's own three feedback comments on the PR (20:30 and 21:25 on 2026-11-14, 02:55 on 2026-11-15):
  - The proving budget should be split into throughput and latency. Only the final aggregation after the transaction set is fixed sits on the critical path: about 0.75 s per merge level on GCP c3-highmem-22, or roughly 2.5 to 3.7 s for a full 60M block on 16 workers.
  - Block validity only needs one valid aggregate covering the declared dependencies. If a builder can't prove something in time, it leaves it out and prices proving through priority fees. So the protocol only has to bound circuit capacity (MAX_DEPS_PER_BLOCK, MAX_INNER_PROOFS_PER_STEP), forced work under FOCIL, and verification cost for mempool nodes.
  - Suggested provisional gas: LEANSPHINCS_VERIFICATION_GAS 3,000 (currently 15,400), LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS 30,000 (currently 572,000), RECURSIVE_PROOF_SHARE_GAS about 2,700 (currently 5,100), LEANSTARK_LEAF_PAYMENT_GAS about 35,000 (currently 675,000), LEANSTARK_CLAIM_GAS about 500 (currently 4,200).
  - Measurements (n2-standard-8, EIP-7870 full-node class): ecrecover 42.8 µs, a leanSPHINCS signature 0.39 ms, a leanSTARK leaf 11 to 16 ms, and the worst block 22.5 ms, about 0.6% of a 4 s deadline. On capacity, a full proof of 16,384 dependencies finished in 2.08 s on c3-highmem-22. Max signatures per leaf: 7 for ML-DSA-44, 30 for Falcon-512, 256 for SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128s.

**Why it matters**
- Whether post-quantum accounts are practical for everyday use comes down to how much gas each transaction pays for signature verification. At today's provisional values a single leanSTARK verification costs 572,000 gas, roughly the price of a few dozen plain transfers. The measurements show verification itself is cheap; most of the cost is rationing builder proving capacity, which the priority fee market can handle.
- The approach is "bound it with caps, keep gas small". That changes how wallets will estimate gas, and it ties directly into whether FOCIL stays: inclusion list transactions carry covering proofs, so builders only have to merge them.
- The author is explicit that the two verification charges are still estimates and need measured mempool verification costs before they're fixed, so don't treat these numbers as final.

**What to watch**
- The EIP-8500 filename and Magicians thread updates, plus the full data from the calibration suite in [NethermindEth/lean-bindings #13](https://github.com/NethermindEth/lean-bindings/pull/13).
- [The PR adding leanSPHINCS to EIP-8141](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12616) was updated again at 03:33 on 2026-11-15 and is still unmerged. Which post-quantum signature Frame transactions adopt determines the real load on EIP-8500.

### 2. Account abstraction: EIP-8151 mempool rules and verifying EIP-7702 delegations
**What happened**
- [EIP-8151 public mempool rules PR approved by a reviewer](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12653) At 02:59 on 2026-11-15, colinlyguo suggested strengthening the rationale: for the "any other digest" case the risk isn't just signature reuse. Given a known public key and an attacker-chosen digest, an attacker can build a (digest, v, r, s) tuple that recovers the target address without the private key. At 03:08 the review concluded "lgtm, only a rationale supplement". The PR isn't merged yet.
- [EIP-8477 (recent block bound delegation authorizations) discussion](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8477-recent-block-bound-delegation-authorizations/30269) At 16:46 on 2026-11-14, Helkomine argued against adding features to EIP-7702: its goal is to move EOAs gradually toward smart accounts, and ECDSA will be phased out in a post-quantum world, so there's little reason to keep hardening it.
- [Magicians: checking that a deployed contract still runs the audited commit](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/checking-that-a-deployed-contract-still-runs-the-audited-commit/30318) At 22:17 on 2026-11-14, thomasharris noted that eth_getProof can prove an EIP-7702 account's codeHash (that is, 0xef0100||address) at a given block, but redelegation emits no event. At 01:01 on 2026-11-15, Zealynx added that an account can switch away and back within one block, so scanning codeHash per block can miss it. Comparing the nonce at both ends of the period is more reliable, since every applied authorization bumps the nonce by one.

**Why it matters**
- EIP-8151 makes ecRecover depend on the recovered address's code, so nodes need to know which account changes can invalidate a Frame transaction. The attack the reviewer described shows why the "other digest" category needs its own quota.
- For wallets and auditors working with 7702 delegations, "same nonce at both ends means no redelegation" is a cheap, practical check you can drop straight into a monitoring script.

**What to watch**
- Whether Frame Transaction Breakout #8 on 11-17 ([pm #2295](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2295)) discusses merging EIP-8151.

### 3. Post-quantum wallets and the gas repricing timeline
**What happened**
- [ERC-8499 discussion: mnemonic fingerprints and stateful keys](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8499-hash-based-key-derivation-from-bip-39/30375) At 16:16 on 2026-11-14, SergeevDmitry confirmed the author had added "keep deriving old key types" to the restore section. At 01:04 on 2026-11-15, Zealynx suggested fixing the fingerprint format and computing it from a public key (for example account 0's SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s key), the way BIP-32 takes its fingerprint from the master public key. If it's computed straight from the seed, anyone holding the paper with the fingerprint can test passphrase guesses offline at PBKDF2 speed. For stateful keys he backs picking leaves at random, because a device reset or a rolled-back flash makes a counter repeat.
- [ACDT #103 agenda](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2299) updated at 16:30 on 2026-11-14. At 21:00 on 11-16, MariusVanDerWijden gives the EIP-8037/8038 status, ChainSecurity and Dedaub present independent audit findings, and the EF covers its internal investigations and app team outreach, including compatibility PoCs for the Juicebox protocol. The call also picks one of two consensus-specs PRs for payload binding, to unblock the consensus spec release.
- [ACDE #249](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285) is at 21:00 on 11-19 with Glamsterdam and Hegotá on the agenda; [ACDC #192](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2305) was created at 16:11 on 2026-11-14 for 21:00 on 11-26.

**Why it matters**
- Fingerprint design sounds like a detail, but it ends up printed on users' backup paper. Get it wrong and the backup becomes a tool for checking passphrase guesses.
- EIP-8037/8038 change the gas cost of state reads and writes, and Juicebox being named shows deployed contracts face real compatibility risk. ACDE #249 will use the findings to decide between revising the spec and scheduling the Hoodi testnet.

**What to watch**
- Which contract types the 11-16 ACDT audit findings flag as affected, and whether the spec needs changes.
- Contract developers can already check [glamsterdam-gas-invariants](https://github.com/ethereum/glamsterdam-gas-invariants) to see whether their contracts rely on gas assumptions that may change.
