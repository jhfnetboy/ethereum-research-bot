## Key topics

### 1. The privacy debate
**What happened**
- [gakonst: the main thing he thinks about is how dead privacy is from here on](https://x.com/gakonst/status/2101382182799552834), 01:45.
- [Vitalik: it's only dead if you give up; I'm not giving up on privacy, I'm doubling down](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2101414022839218479), 03:52.
- [pcaversaccio: privacy is not dead, we have just built the wrong defaults](https://x.com/pcaversaccio/status/2101604296290607327), 16:28; any privacy must be part of the runtime (an execution layer, a browser and so on) rather than something users configure, which is why he has argued for years that L1 must ship privacy (the post is long, and only the visible part is cited here).
- [Derek Chiang: thinking about privacy in counterfactuals](https://x.com/decentrek/status/2101683309004968342), 21:42, imagining Ethereum transactions had been private from day one and a 2027 upgrade took privacy away, and asking how the community would react.

**Why it matters**
- This matches the Hegotá direction: frame transactions, keyed nonces and recent roots together make privacy a default L1 capability. For wallets, privacy by default means integrating features such as privacy pools directly rather than as an advanced option.
