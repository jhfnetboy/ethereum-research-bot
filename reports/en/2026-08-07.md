## Key topics

### 1. Access sets and gas pricing
**What happened**
- [New EIP: persist warm access sets across reverts](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12128) (rakita), submitted, with a [Magicians thread](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8374-persist-warm-access-sets-across-reverts/29341) at 16:00. It amends EIP-2929 so `accessed_addresses` and `accessed_storage_keys` are not rolled back on call-frame revert or exceptional halt. The PR's reasoning: a revert does not evict data already loaded into client caches, so pricing should match actual cost, and append-only access sets remove the need for journal rollback, simplifying implementations.
- [EIP-8141: link the first reference to each cited proposal](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12121) (AnkushinDaniil), submitted, fixing 19 `eipw` link errors with no normative change.
- [Normalized state gas limit](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12119) (anderselowsson), merged as EIP-8372; its [PFI PR](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12122) was submitted the same day.

**Why it matters**
- For contract developers: with EIP-8374, touching the same address again after a failed try/catch or subcall is charged as warm, making gas more predictable for complex transactions, including batches and frame transactions.

### 2. ePBS and slot time
**What happened**
- [New EIP: ePBS mandatory burn of execution rewards](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12130) (benaadams), submitted, now EIP-8375. It burns a configured fraction of aggregate priority fees on every executed payload, including self-builds, and treats the same fraction of an external builder's `gross_value` as an auction burn target.
- [Barnabé Monnot: thanks for the questions and support during yesterday's presentation](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2085634797083165179), 14:50. One highlighted point is identifying deployed contracts that rely on slot time, in joint work with Sourcify.
- [A series of PRs from nero_eth](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12123) highlights each EIP's benefit up front, including the EIP-8131 block-size cap and [EIP-8146 sidecars](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12124), all merged that day; PRs putting the privacy benefit first in [EIP-8250](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12126) and [EIP-8272](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12125) were submitted the same day.

**Why it matters**
- Putting the privacy benefit at the top of keyed nonces and recent roots shows that the main case for both EIPs is protocol support for private transactions.
