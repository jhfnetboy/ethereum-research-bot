## Highlights
1. Frame transactions: Breakout #8 (21:00 on 11-17) looked at how Frames can stay forward compatible with EIP-8288 so wallets don't have to change twice. Afterwards ilitteri opened draft EIP PR #12672, which lets leanSPHINCS signatures travel as "dependencies" beside the transaction and be replaced wholesale by an aggregate proof later. The call agreed to lock down EIP-8151 (restricted ecrecover) and EIP-8298 (SETCODEFROM) first.
2. Verification gas: PR #12644, which raises EIP-8141's MAX_VERIFY_GAS from 100k to 300k, has Besu's approval, but Toni is worried about the builder's critical path. Nethermind measured that once the builder only runs the IL entries the fill rule admits, the worst case is 0.20 to 0.29 s per slot and no longer grows with the cap.
3. Hegotá scope: soispoke wants to accelerate pqai without freezing everything else, with FOCIL in Hegotá covering a limited set of frame transactions. A leanSPHINCS signature is about 5 to 7 kB and an IL is capped at 8 kB, so at most about 16 PQ transactions per block could be forced in.
4. Post-quantum accounts in practice: the ERC-8403 author moved per-device permission checks into VERIFY. Eight kinds of overreach that used to get included and then revert are now all rejected at validation, for only about 0.3k extra gas on a normal transaction.
5. Privacy: ethereum.org launched a guide to institutional privacy and compliance (from EthSystems, formerly the EF's institutional privacy team); Aztec's Nulo wallet extension went live and tells you whether a send stays private before you confirm; Vitalik urged Canadians to push back on encryption backdoors in bill C-22.

## Key topics

### 1. Frame Transaction Breakout #8: forward compatibility with EIP-8288 and a draft for leanSPHINCS dependency frames
**What happened**
- The [Frame Transaction Breakout #8 summary](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/frame-transaction-breakout-8-nov-17-2026/30274/2) went up at 23:06 on 2026-11-17 (an auto-generated Zoom summary that mangles names like Hegotá, Besu and leanSPHINCS), along with the [recording](https://youtu.be/K7LSqIMjo2w). Key points:
  - lightclient recapped three topics from the Devcon Mumbai session: EIP-8288 aggregation, how EIP-7906 interacts with Frames, and whether transactions should move to SSZ hashing and serialization. To be forward compatible with 8288, Frames may need to drop the signature list and add a new frame mode. The aim is to settle these bigger changes within a month, before the year-end holidays.
  - Iván proposed splitting 8288 into smaller pieces and starting with a "links only" dependency frame for PQ signatures, shaped so it stays compatible with full 8288 and users won't need new wallets later. Toni suggested separating signatures into a sidecar, much like Bitcoin's SegWit. lightclient worried about the complexity and argued that unfinished aggregation shouldn't hold back shipping PQ signatures to users. Justin backed 8288 and aggregation but opposed bringing SSZ into transaction routing.
  - On whether leanSPHINCS needs a precompile or can just run in the EVM, the group agreed no precompile is needed if leanPCS stays cheap enough. Toni pointed out that the calldata used for verification doesn't count toward the 100k mempool limit. johba37 shared the gas and signature-handling issues he hit while trying leanSPHINCS on a devnet.
  - Priorities: lock the specs for EIP-8151 (restricted ecrecover) and EIP-8298 (SETCODEFROM, for migrating to PQ accounts) first, while leaving room for a forward-compatible native PQ signature. A slimmed-down devnet without FOCIL came up but wasn't decided. Spencer said his team will focus on Frames and PQ for the next two weeks.
  - Follow-ups: lightclient will work out how leanPCS fits into the frame envelope, decide on SSZ (target: within a month), and handle how EIP-7906 interacts with paymaster transactions in the public mempool. Mario will open a PR with a fixture format for mempool validation reference tests, and client teams will deliver benchmarks for a 300k verify gas cap.
- [Draft EIP: dependency frames for leanSPHINCS signatures (PR #12672)](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12672), opened by ilitteri at 21:14 on 2026-11-17:
  - The dependency frame runs no code and only declares (scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash). leanSPHINCS is the only scheme, and this frame is the only place a signature is declared.
  - Signatures aren't part of the transaction itself. They travel beside it in the mempool and in the block, and every node checks them before execution.
  - A later upgrade can replace the per-signature witnesses with a single aggregate proof, as EIP-8288 does, or add proofs as dependencies, without touching transactions or accounts. For now it only has the abstract and motivation; the spec, an EIP number and a discussion thread are still to come. CI flagged formatting errors on the PR at 06:24 on 11-18.
- [johba37's note on the call agenda](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2295#issuecomment-6948390788) at 19:44 on 2026-11-17: on the Daisugi devnet, checking a keccak-based leanSPHINCS signature in the EVM costs about 60k gas in VERIFY, and one-time WOTS keys cost 64k to 66k. Two signers don't fit under the 100k cap. He also found two places where the devnet differs from the EIP text.
- [ethrex on EIP-8288 and post-quantum signatures](https://x.com/ethrex_client/status/2122761543292993728) at 01:39 on 2026-11-18: LambdaClass's ethrex will help implement and test the execution-layer changes needed for new user signatures and publish benchmarks so wallet and client teams can weigh the tradeoffs. It backs the goal of being post-quantum ready by the end of 2028.

**Why it matters**
- This is the key transaction-format decision for moving accounts to post-quantum. If the PQ signatures that ship now have the same shape as the later aggregation scheme, wallets and smart accounts only have to migrate once.
- With EIP-8151 and EIP-8298 at the top of the list, two account-side pieces of the Hegotá devnet get settled first: ecrecover only works for accounts with no code or a 7702 delegation, and existing accounts can switch to PQ validation logic through SETCODEFROM.

**What to watch**
- The full spec for PR #12672 (how signatures travel on the network and in blocks) and its discussion thread, plus lightclient's conclusions on SSZ and leanPCS integration.
- Wallet and smart account teams: going by johba37's numbers, verifying one hash-based signature in the EVM already takes about 60k gas, so multisig designs have to wait for a higher cap or aggregation.

### 2. Verification gas and FOCIL: two constraints on bringing frame transactions into Hegotá
**What happened**
- [EIP-8141 PR #12644: raise MAX_VERIFY_GAS from 100k to 300k](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644). The PR's rationale: a groth16 privacy pool needs 233,000 to 235,800 gas for its validation prefix, and hash-based signature verifiers written in EVM code need 101k to 223k in a VERIFY frame. Per the Nethermind benchmark, about 100 failing transactions per second per peer at a 300k cap adds a worst case of 24 to 25 ms to block building, exactly the 25 ms budget.
  - At Breakout #8, Besu said it's on board, though its performance team still needs to review. Toni noted that a higher cap affects both the mempool and the builder's critical path.
  - [AnkushinDaniil's reply](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644#issuecomment-6961426269) at 04:24 on 2026-11-18 brought Nethermind measurements. The builder used to execute every IL entry, VERIFY included. Under EIP-7805's byte limits (16 ILs × 8 KiB, 2,624 minimal frame transactions per slot), going from a 100k to a 300k cap took per-slot time from 223 ms to 670 ms with a cheap loop, or from 3.2 s to 9.7 s with KECCAK256 over 4 KiB. Running only the entries EIP-8369's fill rule admits brings the worst case to 0.20 to 0.29 s per slot, and it no longer grows with the cap. He's still worried that tail truncation can leave a transaction unprotected even though it's in an IL.
- [soispoke: thoughts on pqai, Hegotá and FOCIL](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2122724543169253444) at 23:12 on 2026-11-17 ([Magicians version](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/thoughts-on-pqai-hegota-and-focil/30421) at 23:10):
  - Speed up pqai but work in parallel. Glamsterdam is already delayed and the main work is making sure contracts don't break under repricing; Hegotá is in devnets, and FOCIL and Frames are already specced; the PQ specs and cryptography aren't frozen, and on the consensus side nobody has settled whether "PQ consensus" means PQ Gasper or PQ Decoupled Consensus. He argues that removing FOCIL and other features (DFI) before the direction is clear only buys a false sense of security and hurts ACD's credibility.
  - The execution layer needs Frames for PQ, and there's no reason to settle for yet another transaction type. In Hegotá, FOCIL should sit in the middle: cover a limited set of frame transactions with clear IL bounds, starting with minimal FOCIL on devnets and widening coverage step by step.
  - The numbers: a leanSPHINCS signature is about 5 to 7 kB and an IL is capped at 8 kB, so each IL can probably hold just one PQ transaction, or 16 per block (one per includer). Carrying 8288 proofs of around 300 kB in ILs later would need changes to FOCIL. Swapping IL BLS signatures for leanXMSS would grow each IL from 8 kB to 10.4 kB, but a forged IL signature can at worst switch FOCIL off for that slot, so it isn't the most urgent piece.
- Discussion on [draft EIP PR #12663: FOCIL omission witnesses for frame transactions](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12663): at 16:16 on 2026-11-17 soispoke suggested simply pointing to EIP-8369's notion of "position-stable" transactions, and warned that with a shared paymaster a builder can send its own sponsored transactions first and drain the balance. At 03:13 AnkushinDaniil proposed that the paymaster keep a "released" counter that only goes up, stored in the upper 96 bits of slot 0 so no new storage slot is needed.
- [jstinhw's proposal on the breakout agenda](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2295#issuecomment-6941827192) at 14:36 on 2026-11-17: split verification into a NON_STATE part (signature or proof, checked once when the IL arrives and cached) and a STATE part (key binding, nonces, roots, payer). That would remove the builder-chosen index from AA-VOPS and let frame transactions use the same end-of-block check. The call agreed to keep discussing it async.
- The [FOCIL Breakout #46 summary](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/focil-breakout-46-november-17-2026/30100/2) went up at 22:07 on 2026-11-17 ([EIPsInsight recap](https://x.com/EIPsInsight/status/2122723180662104229) at 23:06): consensus-specs PR #5769 got the green light, with a merge planned for the next call; the group discussed IL timing, the retention window (#5785 extends it from 2 to 3 slots) and proposer deadlines. Next week's [FOCIL Breakout #47](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2308) (11-24) is dedicated to PQ FOCIL.

**Why it matters**
- How much a smart account or privacy protocol can do in VERIFY depends directly on MAX_VERIFY_GAS. 100k can't fit a groth16 privacy pool or multiple hash-based signatures; 300k covers most of the schemes measured so far.
- Whether FOCIL protects frame transactions decides whether AA and PQ accounts get the same censorship resistance as ordinary transactions. By soispoke's math, forced-inclusion capacity for early PQ transactions will be tight.

**What to watch**
- Client benchmarks for the 300k cap, and whether PR #12644 gets merged.
- What ACDE #249 and later ACD calls decide about Hegotá's scope, including whether FOCIL stays.
- Apps that rely on paymasters: the censorship risk for shared payers under FOCIL is still being worked out, so don't assume sponsored transactions will always be force-included until it's settled.

### 3. Post-quantum accounts and validator migration: ERC-8403 ships permission tiers, PQ material registration
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: ERC-8403, an account as one hash](https://ethresear.ch/t/erc-8403-an-account-as-one-hash-and-post-quantum-keys/26147/5) at 13:21 on 2026-11-17. johba took up yesterday's "management bit" idea, built a third version of the account and ran it on Daisugi next to the old one on the same day:
  - Permissions are stored per device. The key derived from the seed phrase can sign any frame; a device added with OWN_TREE can only enroll for itself; a device added without it can't send a SENDER frame to the account; one-time keys have no management rights. VERIFY checks every frame using FRAMEPARAM and FRAMEDATALOAD.
  - Eight attempts, including a device revoking another device and a transfer plus a revoke in one transaction, all came back "validation-prefix simulation failed" and the nonce didn't move. On the old account the same day, those attempts were included and cost 240k gas each with a device key, or 103k with a one-time key.
  - Cost: about 0.3k extra gas in VERIFY for a normal transaction, plus about 114 gas per additional SENDER frame, or about 7k at 62 frames. TXPARAM 0x09 and FRAMEDATALOAD behaved on the prototype as the EIP describes.
  - [AnkushinDaniil replied](https://ethresear.ch/t/erc-8403-an-account-as-one-hash-and-post-quantum-keys/26147/6) at 01:39 on 2026-11-18 that Slots16 accounts can change their code, so he'll keep them on target checks only, without selector checks.
- [Magicians: an independent check of ERC-8403](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8403-account-authority-lifecycle/29570/14) at 20:54 on 2026-11-17. it44 recomputed the SLOTS16 leaf, node and root vectors with a separate keccak256 implementation and every one matched, but found the reference account was missing the authorityRoot() function and AuthorityStateChanged event the spec requires. The author [replied at 01:39](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8403-account-authority-lifecycle/29570/15) that both are now added, with a test.
- [EIP-8483, post-quantum material registration (PR #12609)](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12609#discussion_r4598334944) got a review at 17:32 on 2026-11-17. The EIP lets a validator's withdrawal address register 32 bytes of PQ material, stored in the beacon state for a future migration fork. The reviewer pointed out that whoever owns the withdrawal address often isn't the operator (contracts, custodians, delegated staking) and suggested adding a path triggered by the BLS key. Otherwise contracts need upgrades, custodians need to support a new transaction, and delegators need to coordinate with operators, all of which would slow the migration.

**Why it matters**
- The ERC-8403 numbers show that putting permission checks inside VERIFY on frame transactions is workable and cheap, so multi-device PQ accounts don't have to live with overreaching operations getting included and only then reverting.
- EIP-8483 is validator-side pre-registration. Staking providers and custodians should check whether they can send a registration transaction from the withdrawal address.

**What to watch**
- Whether the ERC-8403 PR adopts the v3 permission model, and whether EIP-8483 adds a BLS path.
- [PQ-CL Breakout #2](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2310) tonight at 21:00. jtraglia says these calls are coworking sessions every Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, focused on whatever consensus-layer PQ issue is most pressing that day.
