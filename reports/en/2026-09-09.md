## Key topics

### 1. After frames: recursive STARK aggregation
**What happened**
- [Vitalik: a note on recursive STARK mempools (EIP-8288)](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2097711433073172837), 22:39; he hopes this EIP gets into I* and describes it as the next step after frames, unlocking extreme amounts of power.
- [New EIP: frame type for PQ signature and STARK aggregation](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11772) (vbuterin) was merged. Transactions declare signatures and STARKs (for privacy or for L2s) as "dependencies", and the mempool and block builder can replace them with a recursive STARK proving they all exist, supporting them very gas-efficiently in a post-quantum world. An [EIP-8288 update](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12308) was also merged.

**Why it matters**
- Post-quantum signatures are large and expensive to verify, so putting each one onchain is unsustainable. Aggregation spreads the cost across a block, and it applies equally to proof verification for privacy protocols; it is key to whether frame transactions can carry PQ accounts.

### 2. Glamsterdam's gas capacity
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: from 60M to 200M, simulating Glamsterdam's fee market](https://ethresear.ch/t/from-60m-to-200m-simulating-glamsterdam-s-fee-market/25957), 14:43 (misilva73). Around 21 days of historical mainnet transactions were replayed under the new gas schedule to simulate the first 7,200 blocks (about a day) under 12 demand scenarios. The limit takes about 4 hours to reach 200M; the day shows an initial fee spike, a decline as capacity expands, then adjustment around the new 100M target. Nine scenarios end with median utilization near target and median base fees from 0.0002 to 0.3928 gwei; the other three stay below target even with a nearly negligible base fee.
- [EIP-7928: amend the storage-read gas-feasibility check](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12277) was merged, since the old check could reject valid blocks.

**Why it matters**
- For apps and wallets, fees may rise and then fall in the first hours after the upgrade, and stay very cheap afterward in most scenarios. Gas estimation and fee UIs should be ready for that swing.
