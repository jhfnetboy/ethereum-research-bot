## Key topics

### 1. Wallets and signing: what you sign is what executes
**What happened**
- [ERC-8265: Prepared Transaction Envelope](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8265-prepared-transaction-envelope/28557), 15:40 (mike-diamond), with [ERCs #1753](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1753) submitted: an offchain envelope that carries a prepared but unsigned transaction, batch or signature request from the producer that prepared it (a relayer, DeFi UI, intent solver, multisig coordinator or hardware wallet companion) to the consumer that signs it.
- [EIP draft: Transaction Event Manifest, a signed, protocol-enforced declaration of what a tx may emit](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-draft-transaction-event-manifest-a-signed-protocol-enforced-declaration-of-what-a-tx-may-emit/28558), 17:29 (zergity): today users commit to calldata, not outcomes, and a malicious frontend, proxy upgrade or state drift can produce a result different from the wallet's simulation; the draft has the protocol bind execution to the events declared at signing.
- [koeppelmann: exchanges ask you to "prove" wallet ownership with a screenshot of the Safe interface, which proves nothing](https://x.com/koeppelmann/status/2056384953978360122), 21:42.

**Why it matters**
- For wallets: blind signing and post-signature tampering are the most common paths to losing funds; a standard envelope tells the signer where a transaction came from, and an event manifest gives "what you see is what you get" a protocol guarantee.
