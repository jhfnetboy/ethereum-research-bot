## Key topics

### 1. Broadcasting and asserting private transactions
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: strict role alternation, reciprocal broadcast without relayers for EVM shielded pools](https://ethresear.ch/t/strict-role-alternation-reciprocal-broadcast-without-relayers-for-evm-shielded-pools-spec-population-simulation-no-code-yet/26051), 14:07 (atoranzo, spec and simulation only). Shielded pools depend on relayers because `msg.sender` must pay gas from a funded transparent account; the cited Railgun measurement shows 1,049 self-broadcast withdrawals linked to their depositor through the gas payer and 124 relayer-like addresses serving 89% of relayed volume. The proposal adds a role bit to each note commitment so users broadcast for one another.
- [EIP-7906: per-topic-value event views (TXDIFF 0x0B/0x0C)](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12358) (AnkushinDaniil, merged on the 21st). An assertion protecting a user filters events by that user's identity, such as every `Transfer` whose `from` is the user; under dynamic routing the emitting contracts are unknown at signing time, so keying by emitter is not enough.

**Why it matters**
- Both point to problems frame transactions can solve: gas for private transactions can be handled by in-protocol sponsorship, and transaction assertions let users bound the outcome when signing. Until then, reciprocal broadcast is a stopgap that needs no protocol change.
