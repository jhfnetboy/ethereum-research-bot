## Key topics

### 1. Cross-model collaboration on AA standards
**What happened**
- [ERC-7562 moves to Review](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1804) (forshtat) was merged; it holds the validation rules for ERC-4337 account abstraction.
- [EIP-8141: optional sender-keyed authority read for a shared authorization step](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12340) (AnkushinDaniil, later closed). A `VERIFY` frame can only read `tx.sender` storage today, so it cannot read an authority record in a keystore contract that is keyed to the account and writable only by it, which blocks keystore and frame accounts from sharing an authorization step.
- [EIP-8130: optional model-neutral change digest](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12341), where the signed digest binds only domain, chainId, account, actorId and the old and new config, with no signer identity or transaction, so the same signature authorizes the same change through 8130, another native AA model, or another chain.
- [EIP-7906: abstract covers all three opcodes and the POST_TX frame mode](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12329), one of three updates merged.

**Why it matters**
- After the collaboration broke down, some are trying to let the two AA systems at least share key records and change signatures. For wallets, if these extensions are accepted, users would not need to re-sign every authority when switching chains or account models.

### 2. More uses for recursive STARKs
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: scaling Ethereum with recursive STARKs and the Trustless Log Index](https://ethresear.ch/t/scaling-ethereum-with-recursive-starks-and-the-trustless-log-index/26002), 19:37 (zsfelfoldi). Because PQ signatures are large, EIP-8288's recursive STARK pre-authorization is a very likely upgrade path; then the onchain cost of any statelessly verifiable witness data drops sharply, including log query proofs based on EIP-8304's index tables.
- [ethresear.ch: post-quantum lattice or hash-based](https://ethresear.ch/t/post-quantum-lattice-or-hash-based-one-question-two-right-answers/26003), 21:49, with interviews including Peter Schwabe, co-designer of ML-DSA and SLH-DSA.

**Why it matters**
- Once aggregation happens in the mempool and only one proof is verified onchain, many applications that used to be uneconomical because of proof size, such as log proofs and privacy proofs, become viable.
