## Highlights
1. **Ethlabs' view**: 8141, 8250 and 8272 are A-tier; EIP-8369 (VOPS profiles) is D-tier with a recommendation to reject.
2. **Native privacy**: keyed nonces let privacy protocols store nullifiers as 2D nonces; recent roots let private transactions validate against a recent commitment-tree root in the mempool.
3. **Scope**: Hegotá has 66 proposals, and the next few calls decide which get implementations, devnets and testnets.
4. **gwei names**: kohaku registers stealth keys under .gwei names, with resolution in Ambire and a MetaMask snap.

## Key topics

### 1. Ethlabs ranks Hegotá
**What happened**
- [Ethlabs: we went through what is proposed for Hegotá and wrote up where we stand and why](https://x.com/ethlabs_org/status/2089029459886141611), 23:40; [Barnabé: we published a very detailed snapshot of our Hegotá priorities](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2089032939145429410), 23:53, adding that he will keep pushing faster blocks.
- The piece sets FOCIL as the censorship resistance headliner, and frames native AA as supporting passkeys, sponsored transactions, gas paid in tokens, batching and stronger privacy, with a path to post-quantum keys. EIP-8141 is called the best candidate for native AA, A-tier, partly because a privacy protocol such as Railgun can handle frame transaction validation logic, so users can send private transactions without centralized relayers.
- EIP-8250 keyed nonces is A-tier; Ethlabs sees it as conceptually part of 8141 and wants them shipped together, since 2D nonces let accounts send parallel transactions and let privacy protocols store nullifiers as 2D nonces, a special storage that is very cheap to write. EIP-8272 recent roots is A-tier: privacy protocols validate spends against a recent commitment-tree root, but public mempool rules for frame transactions do not allow reading application storage for DoS protection, and recent roots solve that. EIP-8369 VOPS profiles is D-tier with a recommendation to reject, on the view that the verification gas cap it needs belongs in the FOCIL EIP.

**Why it matters**
- Ethlabs argues native AA and privacy as one package: only 8141, 8250 and 8272 together let privacy apps work without intermediaries.

### 2. Community debate on scope
**What happened**
- [nero_eth: Hegotá is being scoped now, with 66 proposals on the table](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2088855221371891768), 12:07; over the next few core dev calls the list will be narrowed to the EIPs that get implementations, devnets, testnets and a realistic chance of shipping in 2027.
- [kassandra: FOCIL is already a Hegotá headliner, and frame transactions (EIP-8141) should join it](https://x.com/kassandraETH/status/2088867606006571169), 12:57, unlocking native privacy together with keyed nonces (EIP-8250) and root references (EIP-8272); [soispoke: FOCIL 🤝 FRAMES](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2088919736822214716), 16:24.
- [donnoh: recent .gwei integrations](https://x.com/donnoh_eth/status/2088933131143270784), 17:17: kohaku can register a .gwei name and use it to register a stealth key, Ambire can send to .gwei names, and MetaMask has a matching snap.

**Why it matters**
- For wallets: stealth addresses need discoverable receiving keys, and a name system is the user-friendly entry point.
