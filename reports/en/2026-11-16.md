### 1. Glamsterdam repricing: ACDT #103 presents EIP-8037/8038 audit findings tonight
**What happened**

MariusVanDerWijden opens with the current status; ChainSecurity and Dedaub each present independent audit findings; nerolation covers the EF's internal investigation; and EF EVM Dev Tooling reports on app outreach, including Juicebox protocol compatibility concerns and PoCs in [mejango/glamsterdam-pocs](https://github.com/mejango/glamsterdam-pocs). - [Vitalik reposted danceratopz's call to join](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2121835574084723099) At 12:19 on 2026-11-15: app developers are asked to join Monday's call with core devs and auditors to gather evidence on how EIP-8037/8038 affect deployed contracts.
- [Caspar's Ethlabs weekly update](https://x.com/casparschwa/status/2122122477329400278) At 07:20 on 2026-11-16: Ethlabs has spent more than two weeks monitoring, triaging and analysing reports (formal ones and informal ones posted on X) about EIP-8037/8038's impact on deployed contracts, and recommends no Glamsterdam decision until there is clarity. Details are in [Tracking the Glamsterdam discussion](https://ethlabs.org/writings/tracking-glamsterdam.html).
- [ACDE #249](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285) Updated at 06:21 on 2026-11-16; the call is at 21:00 on 11-19 with Glamsterdam and Hegotá on the agenda.

**Why it matters**
- EIP-8037/8038 change gas costs around state access. Plenty of contracts hardcode gas assumptions (a fixed gas stipend for external calls, branching on gasleft), and repricing can change how they behave or even brick them. The Juicebox PoC is a concrete case.
- This is the checkpoint for whether Glamsterdam moves ahead on its current spec. If the audits find widespread risk, the spec could change or slip; if the impact is contained, ACDE #249 can turn to testnet scheduling.

**What to watch**
- Which contract patterns tonight's audits flag, and whether any mitigation is proposed, such as versioned opcodes. [EIP-8488, versioned opcodes for gas repricing](https://x.com/EtherealnewsHQ/status/2122089401157304679), listed in Ethereal news #47 (05:08 on 2026-11-16), takes exactly that approach.
- Contract teams can check themselves against [glamsterdam-gas-invariants](https://github.com/ethereum/glamsterdam-gas-invariants) now and raise anything they find on tonight's call.

### 2. Post-quantum accounts: app-side measurements and a wallet backup format
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: app-side data on the post-quantum ecrecover upgrade](https://ethresear.ch/t/proposed-pq-upgrade-for-ecrecover/25844) At 18:13 on 2026-11-15, johba reported on a Sepolia Safe whose owners only accept hash-based signatures, so no ECDSA key can sign for it, and ran it through the permit flows:
  - Permit2 works today because it asks the account through ERC-1271. The Safe can approve the typed data hash on-chain (265k to 267k gas in Foundry under Prague, versus 85k to 86k with ECDSA owners), or two owners can sign with stateless keys (11.7 KB, and 198k to 241k gas for the verifying contract).
  - EIP-2612 as implemented in OpenZeppelin's ERC20Permit can't work: it only recovers an address, so it fails for every contract account, post-quantum or not.
  - FiatToken v2.2's bytes overloads of permit and transferWithAuthorization go through ERC-1271, so the author expects them to behave like Permit2, but notes this comes from reading the code, not running it.
  - The author's open questions: the proposal brings back "recover only" consumers for accounts bound to a single key, but could it cover a 2-of-3 multisig? Also, one-time keys (WOTS, XMSS) are safe for transactions only because the account ties each key to an on-chain nonce. Off-chain messages have no nonce, so a permit needs a stateless signature, about 5.7 KB per signer.
- [ERC-8499 discussion](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8499-hash-based-key-derivation-from-bip-39/30375) At 16:05 on 2026-11-15 the author johba replied that the fingerprint is now fixed as the first 4 bytes of SHA-256 over account 0's SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s public key (8 hex digits), and is never computed from the root or a seed directly. On a server CPU that key generation took 72 ms against 3.6 ms for PBKDF2, so each guess against a written-down fingerprint costs about 20 times more than PBKDF2 alone. Showing it stays a SHOULD. For stateful keys, the draft now requires a generator seeded from a source that doesn't repeat across restarts, and says a device that can't keep state reliably must not hold a stateful instance. The changes are in [ERCs PR #2172](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2172) (updated 18:10 on 2026-11-15).
- [EIP-8288 amendment PR: committed verification keys](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12667) Opened by Marchhill at 19:09 on 2026-11-15. It defines the generic verification key identifier as a domain-separated hash of a 32-byte program commitment, split out of EIP-8500 for separate review. At the same time (19:10) the [EIP-8500 PR](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12664) got a commit delegating committed verification keys to EIP-8288 and now lists #12667 as a dependency.

**Why it matters**
- Once accounts switch to post-quantum signatures, compatibility depends on how each dapp checks signatures: anything using ERC-1271 keeps working, anything that only calls ecrecover breaks. That's a direct migration checklist for wallets, token issuers and aggregators.
- The gas cost is concrete too: one on-chain approval from a hash-signature Safe costs about three times the ECDSA version, which is the real-world backdrop for proposals like EIP-8500 that aim to cut verification costs.
- ERC-8499's fingerprint will be printed on users' backups. Deriving it from a public key stops it from becoming a cheap way to check passphrase guesses.

**What to watch**
- Token projects still on recover-only EIP-2612 should consider supporting ERC-1271 (for example FiatToken-style bytes overloads); otherwise smart accounts and future post-quantum accounts can't use permit.
- The ecrecover proposal author's answer on multisig coverage, and review progress on the EIP-8288 amendment.

### 3. Account security tooling: admin differences across chains and cancellable transfers
**What happened**
- [Magicians: checking that a deployed contract still runs the audited commit](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/checking-that-a-deployed-contract-still-runs-the-audited-commit/30318) At 19:01 on 2026-11-15, RealSpap (who runs a related project and disclosed possible bias) shared a measurement: the same Safe address 0xcBa28b38103307Ec8dA98377ffF9816C164f9AFa has 10 signers with a threshold of 6 on Ethereum mainnet, and 9 signers with a threshold of 5 on Base mainnet, with replayable cast commands. The takeaway: one shared address doesn't mean the same signers.
- [Draft ERC: protected accounts for fungible tokens](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-xxxx-protected-accounts-for-fungible-tokens/30297) A new reply at 18:59 on 2026-11-15 discussed test cases. The draft (opened 2026-11-07) adds an optional "protected" flag to ERC-20s: small sends stay instant, larger ones are queued and can be cancelled by a pre-registered backup wallet, and a "stress" action sweeps the balance to the backup and locks it, which helps against stolen keys and coercion.

**Why it matters**
- Multisigs deployed at the same address across chains are increasingly common, so monitoring has to check signers and thresholds per chain rather than assume they match.
- Protected accounts put the safety logic in the token, so even a plain EOA benefits. It complements smart accounts (AA), but routers, lending protocols and the like need to handle rejected large transferFrom calls correctly.

**What to watch**
- Whether the protected accounts draft gets an ERC number.
