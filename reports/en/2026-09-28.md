# Ethereum Research Daily 2026-09-28

Keywords: Chainlink CCIP 2.0 first to ship FCR, ACDT #98, 200M gas safe on execution time, Daisugi v0.2 supports frame transactions, EIP-8429 escalating gas for repeated calls, post-quantum stealth addresses

Summary: Chainlink CCIP 2.0 became the first bridge to put the Fast Confirmation Rule (FCR) into production, so transfers originating from Ethereum can move much faster than finality with clear security guarantees; Derek Chiang put it at 30 times faster. At ACDT #98, the 200M gas limit was deemed safe on execution time, and FCR will use a new confirmed block tag. Daisugi v0.2 added frame transaction support, so SPHINCS-signed transactions can run as native frames.

## Key topics

### 1. Fast confirmation goes to production
**What happened**
- [Julian Ma: Chainlink CCIP 2.0 is the first bridge to put FCR into production](https://x.com/_julianma/status/2104586544086389091), 21:58, letting asset issuers bridge at faster-than-finality speed with FCR's security.
- [Derek Chiang: the first project he worked on after joining Ethlabs, rolling out FCR to CCIP 2.0 with Chainlink](https://x.com/decentrek/status/2104591636802179571), 22:18, making transfers and issuance from Ethereum 30 times faster; [fradamt: no more choosing a random number of block confirmations and waiting without clear security guarantees](https://x.com/fradamt/status/2104588393480958066), 22:05; [barnabemonnot](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2104589359445905507), 22:09; [trent_vanepps: a collaborative effort by Ethlabs, CL clients, the EF and others](https://x.com/trent_vanepps/status/2104587770031223170), 22:03.
- ACDT #98 at 21:00 (per Ethereal news #41): Maria Inês Silva found the 200M gas limit safe on execution time; FCR requests a new confirmed block tag rather than reusing the safe tag.

**Why it matters**
- For dApps and bridges, fast-confirmed blocks can be read through the confirmed tag instead of guessing confirmation counts. L1-to-L2 waits drop from more than ten minutes, directly improving the user experience.

### 2. Post-quantum accounts and gas
**What happened**
- [asanso: Daisugi v0.2 supports frame transactions](https://x.com/asanso/status/2104491639251050555), 15:41; SPHINCS-signed transactions can run as native frames with per-frame execution and verification, alongside the existing ERC-4337 flow, with signature aggregation next.
- [New EIP: escalating gas for repeated calls](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12384) (staccDOTsol, later EIP-8429). The client keeps a per-address reference counter outside world state; an account may enroll once, irrevocably, and state-changing calls into it (not `STATICCALL` or `DELEGATECALL`) are then priced on two ratchets, paying the larger.
- [ethresear.ch: PQ anonymity for the Stealth Address Protocol](https://ethresear.ch/t/pq-anonymity-for-stealth-address-protocol/26094), 08:05, and [PQ spending](https://ethresear.ch/t/pq-spending-for-stealth-address-protocol/26095), 08:09 (namnc), the second and third posts in the series.

**Why it matters**
- Frame transactions and post-quantum signatures came together on a testnet for the first time, showing accounts can switch signature schemes. Stealth addresses underpin receiving privacy, and the post-quantum upgrade keeps them secure in the long run.
