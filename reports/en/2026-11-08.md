# Ethereum Research Daily 2026-11-08

Keywords: Glamsterdam debate, do not break contracts, EIP-8487 dual counters, ETH as store of value, FCR

Summary: Core developers argued on X all day about whether Glamsterdam's gas repricing will break deployed contracts. One side holds that you should never break things that work; the other says that would mean the gas schedule can never change. A targeted proposal, EIP-8487, appeared the same day: the old gas schedule governs execution and the new one governs billing. Vitalik explained at length why ETH should focus on being a store of value rather than a fee-burning token. The Ethlabs week 20 update reports that Quicknode now supports FCR.

## Highlights
1. **The "do not break contracts" debate**: fede asks for concrete contracts that would break; Marius stresses that the team is investigating internally with auditors; dankrad argues that "never break existing contracts" is unworkable and that we should instead state clearly which contracts will not break.
2. **Draft EIP-8487**: two counters initialized from the same `tx.gasLimit`. Execution allowances follow the old gas schedule and billing follows the new one; if billing runs out, the whole transaction aborts in a way that cannot be caught.
3. **ETH as a store of value**: Vitalik gives three reasons, one being that optimizing for fee burning would force Ethereum to give up decentralization and censorship resistance.
4. **FCR lands**: Quicknode opened an FCR beta for mainnet endpoints. Keep calling the `safe` tag and get confirmations in about 13 seconds.

## Key topics

### 1. Glamsterdam repricing and "do not break deployed contracts"
**What happened**
- [fede: if we are really breaking contracts, this is wrong](https://x.com/fede_intern/status/2119191942734975150), 05:15: "never ever break working things for users", and he asks for concrete contracts that would break.
- [lightclient: I asked people to check this a while ago](https://x.com/lightclients/status/2119309199788360045), 13:01, and raised it on ACD before that.
- [Marius van der Wijden: read the 20-line blog post before talking](https://x.com/vdWijden/status/2119328227365707994), 14:16. The team is investigating internally with auditors and has opened a contact channel, and will decide how to proceed afterwards.
- [dankrad: "never break an existing contract" is unworkable](https://x.com/dankrad/status/2119332003610776025), 14:31. It would mean the gas schedule can never change and that signature schemes can never be deprecated, which will most likely have to happen. Instead we should be clear about which kinds of contracts will not break.
- [Eli Ben-Sasson: my two cents on the Glamsterdam delay](https://x.com/EliBenSasson/status/2119408292581748897), 19:34. He argues that Starknet becoming its own L1 is even more urgent, and that "upgrading blockchains is hard".
- [kassandra: turn the repricing around?](https://x.com/kassandraETH/status/2119443330673054009), 21:54: instead of raising the gas limit and making a few opcodes more expensive, make most opcodes cheaper.

**Why it matters**
- This debate sets the rules for future upgrades: is gas a stable interface that contracts can depend on? For accounts, the post-quantum migration has to deprecate ECDSA, and if "never break" were accepted, EOA migration would be blocked too, which is exactly dankrad's point.

### 2. EIP-8487: separate execution credits and billing credits
**What happened**
- [EIPs PR #12637: Separate execution credits and billing credits](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12637), opened at 22:58. Both counters start from `tx.gasLimit`: credits use the legacy schedule and govern EVM-visible execution allowances (including CALL and contract creation); billing uses the new schedule and sets the transaction fee. If billing runs out, the whole transaction aborts and the abort cannot be caught, even if credits remain.
- The PR notes that the exact schedules, charge points, refund rules, receipt fields and block accounting are still open, that it is for discussion only, and that there is no client implementation or consensus test yet.

**Why it matters**
- The idea is that the execution allowances contracts see in the EVM keep following the old gas schedule (per the PR, including calls and creation), while the network charges at the new prices. It targets exactly the invariant problem the EF listed on 11-06. The cost is a second way for transactions to fail, so wallet and relayer gas estimates have to satisfy both counters.

### 3. ETH as a store of value, not a fee-burning token
**What happened**
- [Vitalik: why "ETH as store of value" makes more sense to focus on than "ETH as fee-burning token"](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2119401255919604064), 19:06. Three reasons: the fee-burning story was never as strong as it looked; [optimizing for fee burning would require compromising decentralization and capture resistance](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2119401338375430542) (to justify a market cap of about $270 billion, Ethereum would essentially have to act as a world-scale fee platform); and [optimizing for store of value is aligned with decentralization and CROPS](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2119401376753262713).
- [Barnabé: security and utility in a programmable store of value](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2119419778842472822), 20:20.

**Why it matters**
- This supplies the motivation for the post-quantum roadmap: a store of value first needs accounts and assets to stay secure over the long run, which takes priority over L1 fee revenue.
