## Key topics

### 1. Transactions and gas: fixed CPSB in EIP-8037
**What happened**
- [EIP-8037: fixed CPSB plus frame accounting](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11573) (misilva73), submitted: replaces the dynamic, quantized cost_per_state_byte with one constant CPSB = 1174, derived from a 100 GiB per year growth target at a 96M gas reference limit; removes the quantization parameters, adds byte count constants for storage sets, new accounts and authorizations, and adds state gas accounting rules.

**Why it matters**
- For users: a fixed cost makes gas for creating accounts and writing new storage slots more predictable and easier for wallets to estimate; the tradeoff is it no longer adjusts with the gas limit, so changing it later needs an upgrade.

### 2. Accounts: access keys and interop addresses
**What happened**
- [gakonst: access keys are live; the primitive is account delegation, so rather than moving assets to an escrow, give a third party a key to your account with protocol enforced limits](https://x.com/gakonst/status/2048905316126101595), 06:21; [gakonst: this also removes sweeping and sets up the address as the universal interface](https://x.com/gakonst/status/2048905777801576726), 06:22.
- [ERC-7930: Move to Review](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1708) (0xrcinus), submitted and merged; [ERC-7828: Move to Review](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1709) (0xrcinus), submitted and merged.

**Why it matters**
- For accounts: limited keys are a typical native AA use, with permissions enforced by the protocol, carrying less risk than handing assets to an escrow; ERC-7930 and ERC-7828 define interoperable cross chain address formats, and Review means wallets can start integrating.
