## Key topics

### 1. EIP-8037 impact assessment and supporting specs
**What happened**
- [nero_eth: last week core devs delayed the Hoodi fork to study the ChainSecurity report, and over the weekend people worked to get a better picture of the impact](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2114631334526161274), 15:12.
- [Barnabé: a busy weekend letting our agents loose on gas repricings and mechanism changes](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2114409418926465458), 00:31.
- [Ethlabs week 18: vulnerabilities found in Glamsterdam, agents came to the rescue](https://ethlabs.org/updates/week-18.html) (Derek Chiang, published as an X article). EIP-8037/8038 exist to limit state growth: state grows about 116GB a year today, and raising the gas limit from 60M to 200M extrapolates to about 387GB a year. After the ACD call, fradamt and nero_eth each had agents scan for affected contracts, then set up a GitHub repo where agents claim tasks and record findings, joined by several EF and Ethlabs researchers; given their sensitivity, the findings were held for the ACDT call. A second coordination repo targets EIP-8198 quick slots, looking for projects that hardcode 12-second slots and would break at 10 seconds.
- [EIP-8053: gas units at the contract boundary](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12584) was merged, keeping milli-gas inside the EVM and specifying how it works with EIP-8037 and EIP-8141.
- [EIP-8141: charge the EIP-8037 legacy share as extra execution gas](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12588) (LukaszRozmej, a follow-up PR).
- [Magicians: EIP-8456 CALLSTATUS instruction](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8456-callstatus-instruction/30085) opened at 21:54.
- [ladislaus0x: Sepolia has activated Glamsterdam, and with ePBS validators can attest to beacon blocks before verifying payload execution, giving zkEVM provers more time](https://x.com/ladislaus0x/status/2114699748787572767), 19:44; Sepolia's gas limit is already 200M.

**Why it matters**
- Whether the fix changes the protocol (legacy share, reservoir-only credits) or adds tools (RESERVOIR, CALLSTATUS) decides whether deployed contracts must change. Contract teams should check themselves against the EF's gas invariants list first and contact glamsterdam@ethereum.org if affected.

### 2. Privacy and post-quantum
**What happened**
- [EF: glad to support wallet tools advancing privacy](https://x.com/ethereumfndn/status/2114746087382851966), 22:48. After this past week of attacks and exploits, privacy and security cannot be isolated goals; when privacy is the default, you can worry less about attackers using your onchain history against you.
- [lambdaclass: Miden mainnet is live with its first partners](https://x.com/class_lambda/status/2114736381209747672), 22:10.
- [EIP-8288: validate recursive inputs and mempool wrappers](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12590) (Marchhill), bounding recursive proof and list counts, defining canonical wrappers within 2 MiB, and using the elided pooled form for blob transactions.
- [StarkWare: in less than a week the Falcon Challenge cut Falcon-512 verification costs by 50.2%](https://x.com/StarkWareLtd/status/2114756478301315228), 23:30, from 48.4M to 24.1M gas.

**Why it matters**
- Post-quantum signatures are still expensive to verify onchain; costs in the tens of millions of gas show why protocol-level aggregation (EIP-8288) is needed.
