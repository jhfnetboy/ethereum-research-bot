## Key topics

### 1. Shared senders and the mempool for privacy apps
**What happened**
- [soispoke: new post, MATCHA](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2097322172570013894), 20:52, with the [ethresear.ch post](https://ethresear.ch/t/mempool-account-transaction-capacity-from-historical-activity-matcha/25949). EIP-8141 normally admits one pending transaction per sender; EIP-8250 lets many users of a privacy app share one sender without nonce conflicts, which opens the door to admitting several. The risks: a malicious app could submit many valid spends and then invalidate the whole set with one onchain settings change, repeating the revalidation burden; or a malicious user could crowd out an honest privacy app's capacity with low-fee transactions. MATCHA, a local mempool policy, makes every admission beyond the first spend "width" assigned to the sender, earned only from the gas that sender actually used in finalized blocks and not returned when a transaction leaves the mempool. The FOCIL extension for frame transactions (EIP-8369) would make flooding transactions more likely to be included, making the attack costlier.
- [soispoke: new post, Bloom filters and keyed nonces](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2097330892603572323), 21:27, with the [ethresear.ch post](https://ethresear.ch/t/bloom-filters-and-keyed-nonces/25950) (Gottfried Herold). It suggests EIP-8250 tag the nonce type with the first byte of the nonce key, separating one-shot from counter nonces, to stay compatible with future use of Bloom filters to reduce VOPS storage.

**Why it matters**
- Privacy pool users need to share one sender to enlarge the anonymity set, which requires a mempool that can hold several of their transactions at once. MATCHA and the nonce-type tag are two companion designs to make that workable.
