## Key topics

### 1. Glamsterdam repricing: ACDT #103 audit results and Vitalik's position
**What happened**
- The [ACDT #103 meeting summary](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/all-core-devs-testing-acdt-103-november-16-2026/30326) went up on Magicians at 00:03 on 2026-11-17 (an auto-generated Zoom summary, with a few names and EIP numbers transcribed wrong), along with the [recording](https://youtu.be/ltmaae3DFY0). Key points:
  - ChainSecurity reported 152 findings, 76 of which overlap with the EF's internal search, for about 200 in total. Most come from invariants 4 and 7 (gas cost increases), not the reservoir or refill mechanisms. Most are easy to mitigate; a few need contract upgrades or redeployments. They also flagged Gloas proof layout changes and fixed-gas ETH transfers.
  - A second impact study done for the EF identified about 25,000 contracts with non-trivial gas patterns, with 70 medium to high risk findings and 22 contracts the EF's internal study hadn't caught. The team is reaching out to affected protocols in a coordinated way.
  - Rahul from the EF covered the app-side triage: 3 high-severity and at least 25 medium-severity issues, with most teams working on fixes. Marius estimated 10,000 to 15,000 affected contracts out of about 90 million, with a few hundred thousand dollars at risk, and argued the benefits outweigh the risks.
  - Jango from Juicebox and Daniel from Uniswap pushed back. Jango worries about confidence in immutable protocols and about try/catch composability; Daniel says 8037 breaks fundamental gas assumptions and creates hard UX problems. Toni noted that dropping 8037 would block raising the contract size limit. Ansgar argued for shipping the fork as planned to move toward post-quantum sooner.
  - The call didn't decide whether to remove EIP-8037 from Amsterdam; that's for ACDE on Thursday. Next steps: ChainSecurity publishes a public report, the EF coordinates with OP Stack chains and L2s to replay transactions on testnets, app teams can reach out at glamsterdam@ethereum.org, and core devs start documenting protocol invariants, including the ones that will change.
- [Vitalik: my views on EIP-8037](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122192027274321946) at 11:56 on 2026-11-16, with a [Fileverse document](https://docs.fileverse.io/document/dUHG8ythBk3jJuQEjcE7m6). From the screenshots in the post:
  - A comparison of today against "10x gas limit plus the Glamsterdam EIPs except 8037": average block verification time about 20 to 100 ms versus 67 to 333 ms, worst known verification about 2 s versus 2.5 s, state growth about 110 GiB a year versus about 1,100 GiB (in red), and worst-case broadcasting about 1.5 s versus 7.25 s. His note says BALs make sync and write times more tolerable, but nowhere near enough to justify 10x.
  - A shortlist of Ethereum features he expects to change: any gas mechanics (gas limits should be set by whoever loses if they're wrong, not hardcoded, and the ability to give child calls less than all remaining gas may be removed after 8141); the state becoming a partitioned binary tree based on Keccak, with blocks and receipts moving to SSZ; the blob point-evaluation field moving to the 128-bit binary field, with blobs possibly growing to 2^17 bytes; ECRECOVER becoming stateful so accounts that upgrade to quantum safety aren't still exposed through off-chain pre-authorization; a big overhaul of the consensus layer; block.slot no longer working as a timestamp proxy once slot times drop; and transaction formats possibly narrowing to SSZ-serialized EIP-8141 transactions.
- [Vitalik replying on airdrops and state costs](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122211943398449206) at 13:15 on 2026-11-16: airdrops are already advised to use Merkle trees (the issuer posts the root and each user pays to claim), and longer term token balances should live in UTXOs and, for privacy, keyed nonces. In [another reply](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122212649320759507) at 13:18 he says apps will retool toward more computation and less state in the medium run, and execution-heavy apps such as privacy protocols will get bolder about using more.
- [Toni Wahrstätter on the state trade-off](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2122240742114410573) at 15:09 on 2026-11-16: state is growing more slowly than EIP-8037 first assumed, but at a 200M gas limit without the relevant EIPs it would still add about 255 GiB a year. Post-quantum and privacy transactions need more gas, so not raising the gas limit effectively cuts throughput.
- [pcaversaccio's long comment on the ACDT #103 issue](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2299#issuecomment-6913931359) at 17:31 on 2026-11-16 (plus an [X post](https://x.com/pcaversaccio/status/2122277228855836959) at 17:34): don't confuse consensus compatibility with application compatibility. Some of the eight assumptions in glamsterdam-gas-invariants concern execution semantics such as refunds, nested calls, reverts and gasleft(), which can affect control flow and security, and they weren't treated as first-class invariants in the spec and validation process early enough.
- [milonite on create-and-destroy refunds](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2299#issuecomment-6917033995) at 19:56 on 2026-11-16: EIP-8037 still charges the full 183,600 state gas for account creation when a contract is created and self-destructed in the same transaction. CREATE goes from 32,000 to about 195,600 gas (roughly 6x), and whole transactions using the pattern cost 1.6x to 2.8x more. The pattern is used for temporary deposit addresses and similar flows, at about 2,100 contracts a day on mainnet (2026-11-06), up from about 1,200 in August. The original refund section was removed by PR #11611 in May 2026 and the proposal to restore it, PR #12249, was closed. The author asks for it to be reconsidered and notes TCREATE (EIP-8360) isn't in Glamsterdam and can't replace addresses already issued.

**Why it matters**
- This is the evidence base for whether Glamsterdam keeps EIP-8037. 8037 charges for state growth by volume, and Vitalik's numbers say a 10x gas limit is hard to justify without it; the other side's point is that deployed immutable contracts can't be fixed.
- Two direct takeaways for accounts and wallets. Vitalik explicitly lists a stateful ecrecover and a move toward EIP-8141 transactions as expected changes, so products relying on ecrecover for off-chain authorization (EIP-2612 permit, for example) or on older transaction types should plan ahead. And payment or cross-chain products that receive funds at temporary CREATE2 addresses and then self-destruct them will see costs rise noticeably.

**What to watch**
- Whether [ACDE #249](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285) at 21:00 on 11-19 keeps EIP-8037, and whether the state gas refund for contracts created and destroyed in the same transaction comes back.
- Contract teams: follow Vitalik's advice and let the caller or wallet set gas limits instead of hardcoding them, and check against glamsterdam-gas-invariants. Once ChainSecurity's public report is out, go through it line by line.

### 2. EIP-8288 and leanPCS: an aggregation path for post-quantum signatures and privacy proofs
**What happened**
- [Vitalik's long post on going from thinking leanPCS is total bull to being a total bull](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122233780371083553) at 14:42 on 2026-11-16:
  - 8288 aggregates STARKs in the mempool. Users publish a STARK, a SPHINCS signature or a PCS opening; each tick, every node bundles new objects into a STARK over a list of claims and only rebroadcasts claim hashes (about 64 to 96 bytes each) plus one proof. These get re-aggregated into a single master proof per block, and only the claims themselves go on chain, in a dedicated frame type.
  - The reason: after the move to quantum safety, signatures grow from 65 bytes (ECDSA) to 1 to 6 kB (WOTS/SPHINCS) and ZK proofs from 200 to 1,000 bytes to about 128 kB (STARKs), which is unsustainable on chain.
  - Three options. leanSPHINCS supports one purely hash-based signature and gives regular accounts and multisigs quantum safety for little gas (about 4,096 gas for the 64 bytes). leanSTARK is fully general: any signature scheme, multisigs that hide their logic, privacy protocols and "private account abstraction" where one key controls many accounts without linking them, but it needs the protocol to fix an ISA, which will take time to agree on. leanPCS only needs a field (the natural choice being the 128-bit binary field) and acts as a "PCS oracle" from the EVM's point of view, so a STARK can be verified for about 20k to 40k gas. It offers the same generality as leanSTARK, could ship quickly, and leanSTARK can be added later once the ISA is settled.
- [soispoke: a leanPCS EIP is coming soon](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2122274148038672692) at 17:22 on 2026-11-16, since it avoids having to convince everyone to enshrine a new ISA first.
- [Toni Wahrstätter on what EIP-8288 means for privacy protocols](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2122256845079203897) at 16:13 on 2026-11-16: today every privacy transaction carries its own ZK proof and each is verified separately; with leanSTARK/leanPCS they can be aggregated in the mempool and batch-verified once, with less data and less compute.
- [ethresear.ch: benchmarking post-quantum signature aggregation for EIP-8288](https://ethresear.ch/t/exploring-post-quantum-signature-aggregation-for-eip-8288/26261) at 23:33 on 2026-11-16. manel1874 of MultiVM Labs proved verification of five schemes under leanVM (Falcon-512, ML-DSA-44, FORS, Raccoon-128 and Raccoon-G) on an Apple M4 Pro with 24 GiB. The first four fit in a single leanSTARK; Raccoon-G needs about 839 million witness words against a 268 million cap, so it was split into five proofs and aggregated recursively. In single-signature runs only Falcon-512 and FORS peaked below 4 GiB of memory, which makes them the plausible browser candidates. The post sketches a flow where the wallet signs locally and an untrusted external aggregator produces the proof, and notes that LEANSTARK_SCHEME is still reserved in the 8288 draft and MAX_LEANSTARK_DEPS_PER_WRAPPER=1 would need revisiting. The author also cited these numbers in the [EIP-8288 discussion thread](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8288-frame-type-for-pq-sig-and-stark-aggregation/28723) at 23:42.
- [Frame Transaction Breakout #8](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2295) meets today, 2026-11-17, at 21:00. The agenda covers an L1 R&D and Devcon recap, future compatibility with EIP-8288 and leanPCS, devnet status and spec changes, and a call for feedback from wallets, tooling and app developers.

**Why it matters**
- This sets the long-run cost of post-quantum accounts. Signatures no longer go on chain and get verified one by one; wallets can pick their own scheme, including threshold and hardware-friendly ones, while the network verifies one aggregate proof.
- For privacy, the cost of verifying privacy-protocol proofs gets shared out, which fits Vitalik's point that execution-heavy apps will get bolder about using more.

**What to watch**
- soispoke's leanPCS EIP draft and what tonight's breakout concludes about 8288 and 8141 compatibility.
- Wallet teams: the benchmarks show memory on browsers and phones is the bottleneck, so proving locally isn't realistic. It's worth thinking early about what an external aggregator service would look like.

### 3. Frame transaction accounts: mempool rules, permission levels and key recovery
**What happened**
- [EIP-8151 PR #12653 merged](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12653) at 17:09 on 2026-11-16, adding public mempool rules. EIP-8151 makes ecRecover return a result only for accounts with no code or an EIP-7702 delegation, so an EIP-8141 validation prefix that calls it depends on a third party's code. The new rules record each recovered address and its code class as a signer dependency (except tx.sender) and revalidate when the class changes. They cap pending transactions per recovered address in two shares: recoveries over this transaction's signature hash, TXPARAM(0x08), use a reserved share, while other digests get a smaller one, because anyone can replay an old signature. Paymaster recoveries count once per paymaster.
- [EIP-8141 PR #12670](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12670), opened at 17:51 on 2026-11-16, adds pointers to the 8151 rules in 8141's policy summary, direct evaluation and revalidation sections.
- [EIP-8141 PR #12668](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12668), opened by nerolation at 14:00 on 2026-11-16: a SPHINCS account puts its signature in an ARBITRARY entry with an empty msg, and its VERIFY frame reads it with SIGDATACOPY, checks it against the signature hash and its public key, and approves. No new signature scheme is needed.
- [ethresear.ch: ERC-8403, an account as one hash](https://ethresear.ch/t/erc-8403-an-account-as-one-hash-and-post-quantum-keys/26147) at 00:53 on 2026-11-17. johba posted receipt data from a frame account with hash-based keys on Daisugi, which runs Nethermind's EIP-8141 prototype (signatures verified in the EVM, no aggregation):
  - A stateless key (leanSPHINCS's construction over keccak) costs 239,570 gas per transfer, 59,909 for the VERIFY frame, with a 5,684 B signature. A one-time WOTS key bound to the protocol nonce costs 101,984 gas with a 1,417 B signature. With the stateless key, the frames up to VERIFY reserve 75,100 gas, under the 100k mempool cap, but two such checks wouldn't fit.
  - The run showed that when one device key tried to revoke another, the transaction was still included because its VERIFY frame was valid, and only the management frame reverted. As Slots16 stands, any authority can replace the whole set.
  - [AnkushinDaniil replied](https://ethresear.ch/t/erc-8403-an-account-as-one-hash-and-post-quantum-keys/26147/4) at 04:56: the permission belongs in the leaf predicate but has to be checked in VERIFY. He suggests one bit in the slot word that, when set, makes validation refuse any sender frame targeting the account itself, for roughly 2k extra gas.
- [ERC-8499 discussion](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8499-hash-based-key-derivation-from-bip-39/30375), 20:26 to 20:28 on 2026-11-16. Author johba addressed two points. A signature count is state and a phrase restores none, so for key types whose limit could realistically be reached, the phrase-derived key should only certify other keys and each device should sign with its own. And when a restore finds no key in use, the wallet should say which key types it tried and that another wallet might find keys it didn't.
- [Magicians: what to check on a Safe deployed at the same address across chains](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/checking-that-a-deployed-contract-still-runs-the-audited-commit/30318) at 00:58 on 2026-11-17. Zealynx added that owners and threshold aren't the whole picture: an enabled module can call execTransactionFromModule with no signatures, and from 1.3.0 a guard can veto anything the owners sign, so every chain also needs reads of getModulesPaginated and the guard slot.

**Why it matters**
- EIP-8151 is the concrete form of the "stateful ecrecover" Vitalik expects, and the mempool rules close the DoS surface that opens when it ships alongside frame transactions.
- The ERC-8403 data shows what a post-quantum account costs without aggregation (about 240k gas for a transfer with a stateless signature) and exposes the permission problem in multi-device accounts. That cost is the backdrop for the aggregation work in topic 2.

**What to watch**
- Review of PRs #12670 and #12668, and whether ERC-8403 adopts the management bit.
- Teams running multisigs on several chains: add modules and guards to your monitoring, not just signers and threshold.
