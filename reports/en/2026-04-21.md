### 1. Security and governance: freezing exploiter funds
**What happened**
- According to [Ethereal news #20](https://ethereal.news/ethereal-news-weekly-20/), the Arbitrum security council froze the exploiter's ETH.
- [samczsun: a huge day for victims of the KelpDAO hack, and hopefully the day the industry realized it can build useful products while protecting users](https://x.com/samczsun/status/2046455983082267096), 12:08.
- [dankrad: it is great the security council stepped up and stopped North Korea; the interesting question is where the line will be, and he would like crypto projects to establish their own jurisdictions and define how they decide](https://x.com/dankrad/status/2046505104338047421), 15:23.
- [binji_x: you can be happy North Korea did not get $70m for nuclear weapons and sad that trusted human intervention was the only way to stop it](https://x.com/binji_x/status/2046608359072124952), 22:13.
- [ladislaus0x: native rollups inherit Ethereum's security directly, with no multisig to trust, council to lobby or governance to capture, and PoC implementations exist](https://x.com/ladislaus0x/status/2046522102853877829), 16:31; [donnoh_eth: regardless of DVN setup, an OFT using default libraries can be rugged by a 3/5 multisig controlled by LayerZero Labs alone](https://x.com/donnoh_eth/status/2046633638578586021), 23:54.

**Why it matters**
- For users: a council that can freeze an attacker's funds can freeze anyone's, so a good outcome here still shows the final security of many L2s and bridges is people; native rollups and L1 issued assets are the path to less of that trust.

### 2. Privacy and accounts
**What happened**
- [EIP-8182 update](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11551) (RogerPodacter) and [a second update](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11552), each submitted and merged.
- [ERC: canonical verifiers for frame transactions](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1695) (derekchiang), submitted.
- [StarkWare: native privacy is now possible on Starknet](https://x.com/StarkWareLtd/status/2046500492398723514), 15:05; [ethbeat_org: Aztec launched with 4000+ permissionless sequencers and protocol level privacy, a first among L2s](https://x.com/ethbeat_org/status/2046509672102793525), 15:41.

**Why it matters**
- For accounts: frame transactions hand validation to account code, so wallets and the mempool need an agreed set of verifiers to recognize common signature schemes; canonical verifiers spare them from auditing each account's logic.
