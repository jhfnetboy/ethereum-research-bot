## Key topics

### 1. After the SFI: what is settled for 8141 and what is not
**What happened**
- [nixorokish: AA update, the Frame Transactions EIP was moved today to Scheduled for Hegotá](https://x.com/nixorokish/status/2093021853036228665), 00:04. It was proposed as a headliner and moved to Considered in March, and there was strong consensus that shipping AA in Hegotá matters.
- [Derek Chiang: Frames (8141) was SFI-ed only to signal that Ethereum will ship AA in Hegotá](https://x.com/decentrek/status/2093026086552752141), 00:21; it does not mean Frames as written, or even the final EIP number, is set in stone, and he pushed back at ACDE against making the SFI decision now.
- [nero_eth: one overlooked aspect of EIP-8141 is privacy](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2093246965555269899), 14:59. Frame transactions let privacy pools sponsor withdrawals directly from the withdrawal amount, removing the relayer needed today; the next step is STARK aggregation, which can push the cost of privacy pool interactions close to zero.
- [EIP-8141: an opcode and frame-mode registry for the frame transaction family](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12253) (AnkushinDaniil), submitted. The 8141 family (8141, 8250, 8272, 7906, 8288, 8312) allocates opcodes and frame modes per draft, which has already caused two collisions: `0xB5`, assigned by EIP-8272 to `RECENTROOTREFLOAD` and later used by 8141 for `SIGDATACOPY`; and frame mode `3`, assigned by EIP-7906 to `POST_TX` and also claimed by EIP-8312 (UTXO). 8141 would hold the authoritative allocation that dependent EIPs draw from.
- [EIP-8037: restore a frame's state gas on rollback](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12256) (chfast), submitted: the "state gas charged within the frame" in the rollback rules has no counter; it is `L0 - L1 + S1`, and `L0` was never defined.

**Why it matters**
- For privacy: a relayer is a required piece of privacy pool withdrawals today; if 8141 lets a withdrawal pay its own gas out of the amount, one dependency goes away, a concrete use case for 8141's general design. On the spec side, two conforming clients that resolve a number clash differently would fork, and a registry removes that class of problem structurally.

### 2. Post-quantum migration: ecrecover
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: proposed PQ upgrade for ecrecover](https://ethresear.ch/t/proposed-pq-upgrade-for-ecrecover/25844), 22:08 (mirabelenkiy-circle). Many contracts verify ECDSA signatures with ecrecover and are exposed to quantum attacks; rather than disable it, ecrecover could read post-quantum signatures from EIP-8141 frame transactions, with the 65-byte `(v, r, s)` encoding lookup values and a sentinel value triggering the new path. The aim is a graceful migration for immutable contracts, not a fix for EOAs themselves. The author notes the EVM defines ecrecover as a pure function, so any public key lookup raises backward compatibility questions.

**Why it matters**
- For contract developers: permits, meta-transactions and much other signature checking rely on ecrecover and cannot be changed after deployment, so proposals like this decide whether they keep working through a quantum migration.
