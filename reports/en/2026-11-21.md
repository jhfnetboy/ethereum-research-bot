## Key topics

### 1. ACDE #249: Glamsterdam keeps EIP-8037, and the cost of gas repricing comes into view
**What happened**
- [Besu's position on EIP-8037 and Glamsterdam](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285#issuecomment-7002670394), 2026-11-19 14:26. jflo posted two options on the ACDE issue: delay Glamsterdam and make an extremely narrow change to EIP-8037's new "remaining gas can increase" behaviour (sacrificing Sepolia), or move EIP-8037 out of Glamsterdam into Hegotá. Besu prefers the first. It sees broken contracts with hard-coded gas pricing as an acceptable loss, but not broken try/catch-style functions. [Ansgar's reply](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285#issuecomment-7009075594) at 19:26 pointed out a third option, shipping as is, and noted that changing EIP-8037 would only help a minority of affected contracts, since most breakage comes from hard-coded gas pricing.
- [ACDE #249 meeting summary](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/all-core-devs-execution-acde-249-november-19-2026/30071/3), posted 2026-11-20 00:06 (auto-generated Zoom summary; [recording](https://youtube.com/watch?v=_1vhi09peLA)):
  - Rahul (EF) reached out to more than 50 affected companies. With an unchanged spec, mid-January is the earliest they could be ready for mainnet.
  - Ideas such as moving refunds to the end of the transaction came up. Marius explained this would break contracts that rely on mid-transaction refunds and sharply raise gas needs for some use cases. Several people called the current spec "good enough" and said a late change risks new bugs; others worried about the precedent of breaking invariants.
  - Outcome: rough consensus to go ahead on the current spec unless a viable alternative appears. Ansgar pencilled in the week of December 14 (for example December 16 or 17) for the Hoodi fork, with the final go/no-go at the next ACDC. Anyone who wants a spec change gets a one-week mini-sprint to bring impact analysis and auditor views.
- [EIPsInsight's ACDE #249 recap](https://x.com/EIPsInsight/status/2123465286523515114), 2026-11-20 00:15: Glamsterdam proceeds on the current spec with no changes, and Hoodi is tentatively set for the week of December 14. [Full recap and transcript](https://eipsinsight.com/calls/acde/249).
- [ChainSecurity: EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 impact assessment](https://www.chainsecurity.com/security-audit/eip-8037-eip-8038-impact-assessment/), [X post](https://x.com/chain_security/status/2123365533554729248) 2026-11-19 17:39. The EF-commissioned assessment (report completed 11-18) combined AI agent reviews with replays of a year of mainnet transaction traces. It covers 200 findings: 3 High, 30 Medium, 167 Low and none Critical. Affected systems include bridges and messaging, wallets, exchanges, governance, oracles, staking protocols and off-chain services, with consequences ranging from interrupted operations to locked funds. Most can be fixed through configuration; 24 need contract upgrades or migration.
- [Toni on why Glamsterdam kept slipping](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2123748758911607020), 2026-11-20 19:02: it was meant to ship in early 2026 and now mid-January 2027 is the earliest mainnet date. He wrote a long thread looking at why.

**Why it matters**
- EIP-8037 changes state-creation gas costs and accounting (with a separate state-gas reservoir), and EIP-8038 reprices state access. Together they underpin Glamsterdam's capacity increase. Keeping them unchanged means contracts and off-chain services have to adapt to the new gas accounting themselves.
- Accounts and wallets feel this most directly: forwarders, batch executors, cross-chain messaging and relayers with hard-coded gas limits are the most likely to break.

**What to watch**
- The Hoodi go/no-go at the next ACDC, and whether the one-week mini-sprint produces a workable spec alternative.
- Contract and service teams: start by checking code and off-chain services for hard-coded gas budgets, and use the ChainSecurity report as a checklist.

### 2. Hegotá scope and PQ signatures: frames at the centre, FOCIL undecided
**What happened**
- The same [ACDE #249](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/all-core-devs-execution-acde-249-november-19-2026/30071/3) discussed the execution-layer scope of Hegotá, and [Ansgar shared the slide comparing 5 options](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285#issuecomment-7014240900) at 2026-11-19 22:51:
  - The options range from a normal scope down to just the core frames EIP (not even the privacy add-ons for frames). The most drastic one cancels frames and enshrines PQ instead; according to the EIPsInsight recap it got no support, and the rest get two weeks of review. Iván and Justin Florentine favoured a narrower scope, while Łukasz and Ben Adams were fine with a normal scope if the consensus layer is ready.
  - Signature schemes: some argued hash-based signatures (leanSPHINCS) are the conservative choice, while others stressed industry standards, hardware support and user experience. In the transcript lightclient argued users should get several options (including ML-DSA) and worried about forcing everyone onto a non-standard scheme.
  - Next steps: each client team writes a one or two paragraph position by the end of next week; EL devs join the next two ACDC calls to discuss how FOCIL gets decided; no cross-layer devnet until the CL scope is clearer, and the EL devnet pauses new EIPs for the next cycle to harden the current scope, especially frames.
- [Magicians thread: leanSPHINCS dependency frames](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/add-eip-dependency-frames-for-leansphincs-signatures/30448), opened by ilitteri at 2026-11-19 17:59 for [PR #12672](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12672). He also [asked for a slot on the ACDE agenda](https://github.com/ethereum/pm/issues/2285#issuecomment-6992821759) (11-19 04:58). The thread notes that a leanSPHINCS signature is 7,740 bytes and the lasting fix is aggregation, but an account's validation code fixes where it looks for a signature, so the interface has to be settled first.
  - [AnkushinDaniil asked](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/add-eip-dependency-frames-for-leansphincs-signatures/30448/2) at 11-20 21:10 whether it reuses EIP-8288's DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE and triple layout, and whether the scheme can stay a field.
  - [ilitteri replied](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/add-eip-dependency-frames-for-leansphincs-signatures/30448/3) at 22:17 that both are how the draft is being written: a frame valid under this EIP should be byte-identical under 8288. Every triple carries its scheme, so a later scheme only needs one new id enabled by a fork, with no change to the frame or transaction format. Accounts can still verify in the EVM inside VERIFY as the no-fork path.
- The FOCIL debate continued. [Toni's comment on Magicians](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/thoughts-on-pqai-hegota-and-focil/30421/2) at 2026-11-18 13:57 ([X version](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2122949842750119997) at 14:07) argued for doing PQ as "slim" feature forks (Frames, leanVM, DC), bringing FOCIL back once PQ consensus is clearer, and shipping only hardening and simplifications alongside. [Ladislaus pushed back](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/thoughts-on-pqai-hegota-and-focil/30421/3) at 18:17: nobody has shown what deferring FOCIL would speed up, community support for FOCIL has been overwhelming, and reopening SFI decisions now is premature. [fradamt](https://x.com/fradamt/status/2122965773698154776) at 15:10 backed a singular focus on PQ and hardening.
- [EIP-8502: Dynamic individual consolidation incentive](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8502-dynamic-individual-consolidation-incentive/30436) ([PR #12673](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12673)), 2026-11-19 02:11. aelowsson proposes that while the validator count is above target, an incentive is deducted from validator balances each epoch: largest for 32 ETH validators (up to their attestation reward at full participation), falling linearly with effective balance to zero at 2048 ETH, to reduce the load of carrying and aggregating votes. He noted that FOCIL picks includers per validator, which pushes validators to stay unconsolidated. In a [reply at 11-20 16:43](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8502-dynamic-individual-consolidation-incentive/30436/7) he said PFI for the I* fork is the target; gregk mentioned in the thread that EIP-8148 is already CFI'd for Hegotá.

**Why it matters**
- Hegotá has already settled on frames (EIP-8141) as its PQ platform; the scope options are about how much ships alongside frames. That decides when native AA and PQ accounts arrive, and whether FOCIL can give those transactions censorship-resistance guarantees.
- Once the dependency frame interface is fixed, PQ account validation code won't need to change again when signatures get aggregated, so wallets only migrate once.

**What to watch**
- Client team positions on Hegotá scope by the end of next week, and the FOCIL decision over the next two ACDC calls.
- The full specification for PR #12672 and whether it is formally proposed for Hegotá at the next ACDE. Wallet teams should watch whether multiple signature schemes end up supported.

### 3. Verification gas and FOCIL protection for frame transactions
**What happened**
- [PR #12644: raise MAX_VERIFY_GAS from 100k to 300k](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644#issuecomment-6970643954), 2026-11-18 13:08. Toni said 300k is fine and shouldn't depend on FOCIL limits; FOCIL can keep supporting only 100k. He [clarified at 20:40](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644#issuecomment-6980271272) that this assumes it is fine on all clients.
- [AnkushinDaniil's benchmarks](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644#issuecomment-6994859383), 2026-11-19 06:37: on a single host he ran the worst-case frame transaction (300k gas, failing VERIFY, Keccak-heavy) on all 7 execution clients, with median rejection times of 3.1 to 5.3 ms. On [11-20 at 20:52](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12644#issuecomment-7041258616) he asked derekchiang to review. The PR is not merged yet.
- [Draft EIP: FOCIL omission witnesses for frame transactions (PR #12663)](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12663#issuecomment-7015480726), 2026-11-19 23:37. soispoke addressed paymaster risk: as long as the summed max_fee of a sponsor's signed but not yet included transactions stays at or below the paymaster balance minus pending withdrawals, every one of them stays payable at any position. In effect the sponsor applies EIP-8141's mempool rule when signing.
- [ethresear.ch: the networking side of EIP-8288](https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263), 2026-11-18 16:58. cskiraly built a discrete-event simulator for recursive aggregation's networking choices. At 15 signature transactions per second, close to today's rate, other nodes uploaded about 0.4 Mbit/s on average with 2.5 kB signatures or 1 Mbit/s with 7,740-byte signatures, which today's mempool can carry. Aggregation matters for carrying privacy transactions whose STARK witnesses are too big to gossip, and for scaling further.

**Why it matters**
- MAX_VERIFY_GAS sets how much smart accounts and privacy protocols can do inside VERIFY: 100k is not enough for several hash-based signatures or a groth16 privacy pool check.
- Whether sponsored (paymaster) transactions get reliable forced inclusion under FOCIL shapes the experience of sending transactions without holding ETH.

**What to watch**
- Whether PR #12644 gets merged, and the per-transaction and per-IL gas caps in PR #12663 (still to be measured).
- Paymaster teams: following soispoke, keep the summed max_fee of your signed, pending transactions within your available balance.
