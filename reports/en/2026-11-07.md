## Highlights
1. **Data on the native AA validation budget**: a 100k MAX_VERIFY_GAS is not enough for a single Groth16 private transfer (at least 211,828 gas) or for verifying SPHINCS+-style signatures in the EVM (up to about 276k). 300k is workable with a client-side protection, with the cost shifting to the mempool.
2. **A 7702 delegation is a standing instruction**: Trail of Bits notes that once an attacker has a valid signature, the delegation lets funds deposited later be moved too, with no further signatures.
3. **Protected accounts at the token level**: small transfers are instant, large ones are queued and can be cancelled by a backup wallet, and it works with any wallet, including plain EOAs.
4. **Repricing does not mean more expensive**: the gas limit rises at the same time, so state operations do not necessarily get proportionally more costly.

## Key topics

### 1. How large can EIP-8141's validation budget be: a mempool DoS benchmark
**What happened**
- [ethresear.ch: a mempool DoS benchmark for the EIP-8141 validation budget](https://ethresear.ch/t/how-large-can-eip-8141s-validation-budget-be-a-mempool-dos-benchmark/26224), posted at 06:26 by Stefano De Angelis, Daniil Ankushin and Manuel Arto of Nethermind. In a frame transaction the signature check is arbitrary EVM code in a VERIFY frame, and a node must simulate the validation prefix before admitting the transaction. If validation fails, nobody pays for that work, which is a DoS vector.
- Today MAX_VERIFY_GAS is 100,000 gas per transaction. A single-note Groth16 private spend needs at least 211,828 gas even after optimization, and verifying hash-based SPHINCS+ variants in the EVM costs up to about 276k.
- Results: without client protection, block processing on the slowest machine stays within a 25ms delay budget only up to about 15M gas/s, and with Nethermind's limit of about 100 failing transactions per second per connection, the cap has to stay at 100k. With a protection that pauses transaction validation while the node processes a block, 300k is workable: at 100 tx/s (30M gas/s), block processing stays within 50ms even on p90 mainnet blocks. The cost is that during a flood the mempool admits only 12% to 27% of honest frame transactions.

**Why it matters**
- MAX_VERIFY_GAS is the hard ceiling on which accounts native AA can support: privacy pools paying their own gas and PQ signature accounts both hit it. 300k is the first candidate value backed by measurements, at the price of a lower admission rate during attacks.

**What to watch**
- Whether a PR raises the EIP-8141 cap to 300k, and whether other clients reproduce the same protection.

### 2. After Binance: signing systems and account-level protection
**What happened**
- [Trail of Bits: after Binance, check what your signing systems can approve](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/11/06/after-binance-check-what-your-signing-systems-can-approve/) (published 11-06 US time; [Trail of Bits announcement post](https://x.com/trailofbits/status/2118763769593741553) at 00:53). The core point: if a server allowed to request signatures is compromised, a key can be misused without ever being extracted. A 7702 delegation is like the wallet signing a standing instruction, "use this code when my account is called", which stays in place until changed or cleared with the wallet's key, so funds deposited after the first theft were moved too. Four recommendations: reduce access to signing infrastructure; treat delegation as an administrative privilege; limit the damage even if signing is compromised; monitor changes in wallet authority. The post also notes that 7702 authorizations sit outside ordinary calldata, and that session keys, recovery paths, Safe modules and ERC-4337 UserOps all need monitoring too.
- [Magicians: draft ERC, Protected Accounts for Fungible Tokens](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-xxxx-protected-accounts-for-fungible-tokens/30297), opened at 14:28. An optional "protected" flag for ERC-20s: small transfers are instant, transfers above the limit are queued and can be cancelled by a pre-registered backup wallet. A "stress" action cancels all pending transfers and moves the balance to the backup, locked for a preset duration the caller cannot choose, which also helps against coercion. Over-limit `transferFrom` calls revert, so routers and lending protocols cannot be fooled. The reference implementation is a 1:1 USDC wrapper (pUSDC) on Base Sepolia, built at ETHGlobal Mumbai. Turning protection on is instant; weakening it (turning it off, raising the limit, shortening the wait, changing the backup) waits for a delay the holder chooses.

**Why it matters**
- The two efforts complement each other: Trail of Bits covers how institutions manage signing and delegation, while Protected Accounts adds insurance at the token level for any holder, including EOAs that never upgrade to a contract account. What individuals can do: do not sign 7702 authorizations you do not recognize, and keep large balances in accounts with delays and recovery.

### 3. What gas repricing actually does
**What happened**
- [Francesco: some thoughts on gas repricings and their impact](https://x.com/fradamt/status/2119036408006594634), 18:57. Higher gas costs for state operations do not make them proportionally more expensive, because the gas limit is rising too.
- [Barnabé: two classes of "invariants"](https://x.com/barnabemonnot/status/2118997007465324770), 16:20, commenting on an outside write-up: he disagrees with some of its assumptions but agrees with its conclusion that Glamsterdam should proceed. He says there are really two classes of invariants, the first being "parameters" such as the gas cost of an operation (the post is cut off at that point).

**Why it matters**
- For users, gas numbers for account operations (creating an account, a first 7702 authorization, receiving a token for the first time) will go up, but if the gas limit and base fee move together, the money actually spent may not. Fee estimates should look at gas times price, not gas alone.
