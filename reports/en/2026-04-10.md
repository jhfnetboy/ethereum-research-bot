## Key topics

### 1. Roadmap: EF Checkpoint #9
**What happened**
- [EF blog: Checkpoint #9](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/04/10/checkpoint-9) (Protocol Support Team): Glamsterdam is slow but steady; ePBS splits block production into two parties acting in sequence inside consensus, so the protocol must handle disagreement or failure between them, touching nearly the whole stack; gas repricings are prioritized as a bundle and EIP-7954, raising the contract size limit, is likely prioritized too; Hegotá chose FOCIL as headliner and committed to account abstraction as a minor feature, with non headliner EIPs open from April 9.
- [nixorokish: core devs are targeting next week for the first Glamsterdam devnet, with ePBS a structurally complex change](https://x.com/nixorokish/status/2042639203352322486), 23:21.

### 2. Accounts: EOA and delegation proposals for Hegotá
**What happened**
- [EIP-8081: propose EIP-7819 for inclusion in Hegota](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11505) (Amxx), submitted; EIP-7819 is the SETDELEGATE instruction; [EIP-7819 update](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11504) (Amxx), submitted and merged.
- [EIP-8081: propose EIP-7851 and EIP-8151 for inclusion in Hegotá](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/11506) (nconsigny), submitted: EIP-7851 adds a precompile for EOAs with delegated code to deactivate their key, and EIP-8151 makes ecRecover aware of the deactivation; the two are complementary.

**Why it matters**
- For accounts: EIP-7702 turns EOAs into smart accounts but the original key keeps full control; deactivating it completes the move to a smart account and also neutralizes an exposed ECDSA key under quantum threat.
