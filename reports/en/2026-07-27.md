## Key topics

### 1. EIP-8151 and account migration
**What happened**
- [EIP-8151: restrict ecRecover by account code](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/pull/12019) (colinlyguo), submitted and merged: applies EIP-3607's account code restriction to `ecRecover` while preserving the EIP-7702 delegation exception. It complements account migration paths such as EIP-7851 and EIP-8298 by extending protocol-level ECDSA restrictions to `ecRecover`, protecting immutable contracts when accounts move to code-controlled authorization, including post-quantum schemes.

**Why it matters**
- For wallets and contracts: after an account migrates to a post-quantum or other signature scheme, the migration is incomplete if the old ECDSA key is still accepted through `ecRecover` in permit-style contracts. This restriction makes migration take effect, and contracts relying on `ecRecover` should assess the impact.

### 2. Privacy
**What happened**
- [nero_eth: Hegotá (next year) will make privacy applications first-class citizens of Ethereum](https://x.com/nero_eth/status/2081780606808043645), 23:35, removing trusted intermediaries (relayers), aligning them with the needs of partial stateful nodes, and making them post-quantum ready with a path to very cheap mixing through STARK aggregation. [soispoke: let's do it, native privacy on Ethereum](https://x.com/soispoke/status/2081780874324926569), 23:36.
- [Nethermind: the design behind Stellar Private Payments](https://x.com/Nethermind/status/2081721101780975887), 19:39: compliance is enforced by the protocol, and the key choice is that association sets attach to user identities rather than deposits, which enables KYC gating and account-level freezes.

**Why it matters**
- For privacy wallets: removing relayers means users withdrawing from privacy pools no longer depend on a trusted third party to forward their transactions.
